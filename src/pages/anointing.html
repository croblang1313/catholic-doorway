Anointing of the Sick and Last Rites

When someone is seriously ill, facing surgery, weakened by age, or dying, a priest can bring the sacraments of healing and peace. Call early. Do not wait until the last moment.

If someone is dying now

  1. Call the parish phone number. After hours, most parish voicemails give an emergency number for a priest.
  2. In a hospital, hospice or nursing home, ask the nurse to call the Catholic chaplain or a priest.
  3. While you wait, pray with the person: an Our Father, a Hail Mary, or simply “Jesus, I trust in you.” Hearing is often the last sense to go.

What is the Anointing of the Sick?

It is a sacrament for anyone whose health is seriously at risk, not only for the dying. The priest lays his hands on the sick person, prays, and anoints their forehead and hands with blessed oil. It brings strength, peace, courage and the forgiveness of sins, and sometimes physical healing if that is God’s will. It can be received more than once, for example before a major surgery and again if the illness worsens.

Good times to ask for it:

  • before a serious operation,
  • with a serious diagnosis such as cancer or heart failure,
  • when an older person becomes noticeably frail,
  • when someone is near death.

Many parishes also hold an anointing service a few times a year after Mass.

What are “Last Rites”?

“Last Rites” is the everyday name for the sacraments given to a dying Catholic:

  1. Confession, if the person is able;
  2. Anointing of the Sick;
  3. Viaticum, the person’s last Holy Communion, “food for the journey.”

The priest may also give the Apostolic Pardon, a special blessing at the hour of death.

Common questions

My loved one is not very religious. Can they still receive it?

If they are Catholic, yes. Tell the priest about the situation. If they are not Catholic, a priest or chaplain will gladly come to pray with them and the family.

What if they are unconscious?

A priest can still anoint a Catholic who is unconscious, trusting they would have wanted it.

What if they have already died?

Call anyway. The priest will pray with the family and commend the person to God. Then see Planning a Catholic funeral.

Can someone bring Communion to a homebound person?

Yes. Ask the parish. A priest, deacon or trained parishioner can bring Communion regularly to people who cannot get to Mass.
