Bible and Catechism Reading Plans

Read the whole Bible or the whole Catechism in a year, a few minutes a day, or start smaller with the Gospels in 30 days. Begin on any day of the year. Your progress stays on this device, with nothing to sign up for.

365 days · about 20 minutes a day

Bible in a Year

All 73 books of the Catholic Bible. Each day has three readings: a passage from the Old Testament, one from the New Testament, and one from the Psalms or the wisdom books (Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, the Song of Songs, Wisdom and Sirach). The portions are balanced by length, so no day is much longer than another.

365 days · about 10 minutes a day

Catechism in a Year

All 2,865 paragraphs of the Catechism of the Catholic Church, seven or eight a day, in order: the Creed, the sacraments, the moral life and prayer. Each day links to the official text on the Vatican’s website.

30 days · about 15 minutes a day

The Gospels in 30 Days

Matthew, Mark, Luke and John, about three chapters a day. The best place to start if you have never read the Bible: one month to meet Jesus in the words of the four evangelists.

90 days · about 20 minutes a day

OCIA in 90 Days

For anyone in OCIA or thinking about becoming Catholic, and for sponsors walking beside them. Each day pairs a short passage from the Gospels of Luke and John or the Acts of the Apostles with a section of the Catechism, topic by topic: God, Jesus, the Holy Spirit, the Church, the seven sacraments, the moral life and prayer.

How the plans work

  • Start any day. Day 1 is the day you press Start, so beginning in March does not put you behind. Reading with a group? Choose a different start date, or start on January 1 to follow the calendar year.
  • Missed a day? Just keep going. The page tells you how many days you have not marked and takes you to the first one. Many people simply read two days at once until they catch up.
  • Your progress is private. What you mark as read is saved only in this browser on this device. We never see it, and there is no account. If you clear your browser data or switch phones, your progress starts over (the readings do not change).
  • The Bible text. Readings open in our Bible reader, which has the complete Douay-Rheims Bible. Its psalm numbers follow the ancient Greek and Latin Bibles, usually one lower than modern Bibles from Psalm 10 to 147, so we show the modern number in parentheses, for example “Psalm 22 (23)”. You can also follow along in your own Catholic Bible.
  • The Catechism. Paragraph numbers are the same in every language and edition, so you can use a printed copy or the Vatican’s online Catechism. Learn more on our Catechism page.

Before you read, it helps to pray for a moment: “Speak, Lord, for your servant is listening” (1 Samuel 3:9). For a slower, prayerful way of reading a short passage, see lectio divina.
