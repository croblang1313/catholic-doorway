Getting a copy of your baptism record

Every Catholic baptism is recorded in a book kept by the parish where it happened. Later sacraments, such as Confirmation and marriage, are added in its margin. You will need a recent copy for a wedding, and sometimes to be a godparent or to enroll in a Catholic school.

How to request it

  1. Find the parish where you were baptized

    Ask your family, or look at your old baptism certificate if you have one. Search the parish name on our church map or the web.

  2. Contact the parish office

    Many parishes have a request form on their website; otherwise call or email. Give:

    • your full name at baptism and your date of birth,
    • your parents’ full names, including your mother’s maiden name,
    • the approximate date of the baptism,
    • why you need it (for example, “for my wedding”) and where to send it.

  3. Ask for a copy “with notations”

    For a wedding, say you need a recently issued certificate with all notations. Most dioceses want one issued within the last six months. Some parishes mail it directly to the parish that asked for it.

Most parishes provide certificates for free or for a small donation. Allow a couple of weeks.

If it is not that simple

The parish closed or merged

The records were moved, usually to the parish it merged with or to the diocese’s archives. Call the diocesan office (the chancery) and ask where that parish’s records are kept. Find your diocese.

I was baptized in another country

Write or email that parish directly. It can take a while, so start early. Your local parish can sometimes help you make the request.

I was baptized in another Christian church

Ask that church for a certificate. If you are becoming Catholic, your OCIA leader will tell you what is needed.

No record can be found

A sworn statement from someone who was present, such as a parent or godparent, is often accepted. If there is real doubt that a baptism took place, a priest can baptize conditionally. Your parish will help.
