Having your child baptized

Baptism is the first sacrament, the doorway to every other one. The Church asks parents to have their baby baptized in the first weeks after birth. Here is how it usually works in a U.S. parish.

Step by step

  1. Call the parish early

    Contact your parish office while you are expecting, or soon after the birth. Ask about baptism dates and the preparation class. If you have not registered at the parish, they will usually ask you to.

  2. Take the preparation class

    Most parishes ask parents (and sometimes godparents) to attend a short class, often one evening or one session after Mass. If you took one for an older child, you may not need to repeat it.

  3. Choose godparents

    See the requirements below. The parish will often ask each godparent for a sponsor letter from their own parish.

  4. Gather the paperwork

    Usually a copy of the child’s birth certificate, the registration form, and the godparents’ sponsor letters.

  5. Celebrate the baptism

    Baptisms happen during a Sunday Mass or in a separate ceremony, often on a Sunday afternoon.

There is no charge for any sacrament. Many families give an offering to the parish, but it is never required.

Choosing godparents

Church law asks for one godparent, or two (one man and one woman). Each Catholic godparent must:

  • be at least 16 years old,
  • be a Catholic who has been confirmed and has received First Communion,
  • be living a life of faith in keeping with the role (for example, going to Mass, and if married, married in the Church),
  • not be the child’s mother or father.

A baptized Christian of another church cannot be a godparent, but can stand alongside a Catholic godparent as a Christian witness. Choose people who will pray for your child and help them grow in faith, not only close friends or family.

On the day

The ceremony takes about 30 minutes. The priest or deacon asks what name you have given your child and what you ask of the Church; you answer “Baptism.” He traces the Sign of the Cross on the baby’s forehead, the readings are heard, and everyone renews their baptismal promises. Then he pours water over the baby’s head three times (or immerses the baby) saying, “I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.” The baby is anointed with holy oil (chrism), dressed in a white garment, and a candle is lit from the Easter candle.

Bring a white outfit or blanket (some parishes provide a small white garment), and a towel. Photos are usually welcome; ask the parish about its custom.

Special situations

We are not married in the Church, or we have not been to Mass in a while

Call anyway. The Church asks that there be a real hope the child will be raised Catholic, so the priest will want to talk with you. He may invite you to take a step yourself, such as coming back to Mass, but the conversation is meant to welcome you. See Coming back to the Church.

Only one parent is Catholic

That is common. The Catholic parent promises to do what they can to raise the child in the faith.

My child is 7 or older

Older children prepare through a version of OCIA adapted for their age, and often receive Baptism, Confirmation and First Communion together.

In an emergency

If a baby (or anyone) is in danger of death and no priest or deacon can come, anyone may baptize. Pour ordinary water on the person’s head while saying: “I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.” Then tell your parish so it can be recorded.
