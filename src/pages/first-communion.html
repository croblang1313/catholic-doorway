First Holy Communion

Receiving Jesus in the Eucharist for the first time is one of the great days of a Catholic life. Most children receive it around second grade, and adults receive it when they enter the Church. Here is how preparation works and what to expect.

What the Eucharist is

At the Last Supper, Jesus took bread and said, “This is my body,” and took the cup and said, “This is my blood” (Matthew 26:26–28). Catholics believe that at every Mass, through the words of the priest and the power of the Holy Spirit, the bread and wine truly become the Body and Blood of Christ. This is the Real Presence. Receiving Communion unites us with Jesus and with one another, and the Church calls it “the source and summit of the Christian life.”

For children

Children can receive First Communion once they reach the “age of reason,” about 7, and can tell ordinary bread from the Eucharist. In most U.S. parishes that means second grade, after preparation that often begins in first grade. Children make their First Confession (also called First Reconciliation or First Penance) a few weeks or months before.

Preparation happens through the parish religious education program (often called CCD or faith formation) or the Catholic school, with parents helping at home. Many dioceses ask for one or two years of preparation, so call in the spring or summer before first grade.

Step by step

  1. Register with the parish

    If you have not registered at the parish, start there, then sign your child up for religious education or the sacramental preparation program.

  2. Bring the baptism certificate

    Your child needs to be baptized Catholic. If they were baptized in another parish, request a copy of the record. If they are not baptized yet, the parish will arrange that first (see older children below).

  3. Classes and family practice

    Children learn about Jesus, the Mass, prayer and the sacraments. Going to Sunday Mass as a family is the best preparation of all; our Mass companion and Inside the Mass guide help children follow along.

  4. First Confession

    Usually a gentle, special service for the children. There is an examination of conscience for kids to practice with at home.

  5. Rehearsal and First Communion Mass

    Often in spring, around Easter or in May, sometimes with all the children together and sometimes family by family at a Sunday Mass.

There is no charge for any sacrament. Programs may ask a fee for books, and parishes help families who cannot pay.

On the day

  • What to wear. Traditionally girls wear a white dress, sometimes with a veil, and boys a suit or white shirt and tie. White recalls the white garment of Baptism. Simple, modest clothes are always fine; ask whether your parish has a dress guideline.
  • Fasting. Catholics do not eat or drink anything except water and medicine for one hour before receiving Communion. For a morning Mass, a light early breakfast is fine.
  • Photos. Many parishes ask families not to take photos during Mass and arrange a group photo or a photographer instead.
  • Gifts. Common gifts are a rosary, a children’s Bible, a cross or a saint medal. Many families celebrate with a meal afterward.

How to receive Communion

  1. Walk up in line with hands joined. Before receiving, make a small sign of reverence, such as a bow of the head.
  2. The priest or minister holds up the Host and says, “The Body of Christ.” Answer “Amen,” which means “Yes, I believe.”
  3. On the hand: place your left hand on top of your right, palms up, like a throne. Step to the side, pick up the Host with your right hand and put it in your mouth right away.
    On the tongue: hold your head up, open your mouth and put out your tongue.
  4. If the cup is offered, the minister says, “The Blood of Christ.” Answer “Amen,” take the cup, drink a small sip and hand it back. Receiving the Host alone is receiving all of Christ.
  5. Return to your seat and spend a quiet moment thanking Jesus.

Older children and adults

  • Children 7 and older who are not baptized follow an adapted form of OCIA for children and usually receive Baptism, Confirmation and First Communion together.
  • Teens who missed First Communion can prepare through the parish youth program, often privately and quickly. Ask the director of religious education.
  • Baptized Catholic adults who never received First Communion ask the parish about adult preparation, often the same course as adult Confirmation.
  • Adults becoming Catholic receive their First Communion at the Easter Vigil after preparing through OCIA.

Common questions

Can a non-Catholic receive Communion at Mass?

Ordinarily no, because receiving Communion expresses full unity with the Catholic Church and its faith. Non-Catholics are warmly welcome to come forward with arms crossed over the chest for a blessing, or to stay in the pew and pray. (Orthodox Christians follow their own Church’s rules.)

Can my child receive if one parent is not Catholic?

Yes. What matters is that the child is baptized Catholic and prepared, and that the family will support their faith.

My child has a disability or sensory needs. Can they still receive?

Yes. The U.S. bishops ask parishes to adapt preparation to each child. Talk with the parish about one-on-one preparation, a quieter Mass and, for celiac disease, a low-gluten Host.

How often should Catholics receive Communion?

Catholics are asked to receive at least once a year, during the Easter season, and are encouraged to receive at every Mass if they are prepared. Anyone aware of a serious sin should go to Confession first.
