The Catechism of the Catholic Church

Published by St. John Paul II in 1992, the Catechism is the full summary of Catholic belief in 2,865 numbered paragraphs. When people cite “CCC 1324,” they mean paragraph 1324. It has four parts: what we believe (the Creed), how we worship (the sacraments), how we live (the moral life and the Ten Commandments), and how we pray (the Our Father).

Major Church documents

The Popes teach through encyclicals (letters to the whole Church), apostolic exhortations (often after a meeting of bishops called a synod) and apostolic letters. The Second Vatican Council (1962–1965) gathered the world’s bishops and produced sixteen documents that shape Catholic life today. Documents are usually known by their first words in Latin, such as Lumen Gentium, “Light of the Nations.”
