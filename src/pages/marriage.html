Getting married in the Catholic Church

Congratulations! Catholics see marriage as a sacrament, a lifelong covenant that reflects Christ’s love. The most important tip: talk to the parish before you book a reception venue, ideally a year ahead.

Step by step

  1. Contact the parish 9 to 12 months ahead

    Most U.S. dioceses ask for at least six months of preparation, and popular dates fill early. Weddings are usually held at the parish of the bride or the groom. Call and ask to meet with the priest or deacon about getting married.

  2. Meet with the priest or deacon

    He will get to know you, check that you are both free to marry, and explain the parish’s preparation. Mention right away if either of you has been married before (even a civil marriage, even to someone who was not Catholic). See special situations.

  3. Do the marriage preparation

    Expect a few meetings, a questionnaire about your relationship (such as FOCCUS or PREPARE/ENRICH), and a course: a weekend retreat like Engaged Encounter, a parish class, or meetings with a mentor couple. Many dioceses also ask couples to attend a class on Natural Family Planning.

  4. Gather your documents

    See the list below.

  5. Plan the ceremony

    Choose the readings and music with the parish. A Catholic wedding can take place within a Mass or in a ceremony without Mass (often chosen when one spouse is not Catholic). Many couples go to Confession before the wedding.

  6. Get your civil marriage license

    The priest or deacon acts for the state too, so you will need a license from your county. Check how long it is valid and get it within that window.

Documents you will usually need

  • A recently issued baptism certificate for each Catholic, usually dated within the last six months, from the parish where you were baptized. It will list later sacraments in the margin. How to request one.
  • Proof of Confirmation and First Communion, if not noted on the baptism certificate.
  • For a baptized non-Catholic partner, a baptism certificate from their church if available.
  • Statements (sometimes called affidavits of freedom to marry) from you and from people who know you, such as parents.
  • Your civil marriage license.

Special situations

One of us is not Catholic

That is common and welcome. If your partner is a baptized Christian, the priest asks the bishop for permission; if your partner is not baptized, he asks for a dispensation. Both are routine and handled by the parish. The Catholic promises to remain faithful and to do all they can to have their children baptized and raised Catholic. The non-Catholic partner is told about this promise but is not asked to promise anything.

One of us was married before

The Church presumes every marriage is valid until shown otherwise, so a previous marriage (for either of you) must be examined before a new wedding date can be set. This often means asking a Church tribunal for a declaration of nullity, sometimes called an annulment. It does not mean the earlier relationship never existed or that children are illegitimate. Start this process as early as you can, because it can take months.

We are already married civilly

A Catholic married outside the Church can usually have the marriage recognized through convalidation, a simple ceremony after some preparation. Talk with your priest.

We want to marry outdoors or in another church

Catholics normally marry in a Catholic church. Outdoor weddings are generally not allowed in U.S. dioceses. When marrying a non-Catholic, the bishop can sometimes give permission to marry in the partner’s church; ask your priest early.

Common questions

Does it cost anything?

The sacrament is free, but parishes often charge fees for the church building, musicians and staff, plus the cost of a preparation course. Ask for the list early.

Can we write our own vows?

No. The Church uses set vows, because the vows are the heart of the sacrament. You can personalize readings, music and prayers of the faithful.

We live together. Is that a problem?

Be honest with the priest. He will not turn you away. He will talk with you about it as part of your preparation.
