How to go to Confession

Confession (also called the Sacrament of Penance or Reconciliation) is where Catholics receive God’s forgiveness through a priest. It takes about five minutes, and it is much gentler than most people expect, especially if it has been a long time.

When and where

Most parishes hear confessions at set times every week, often on Saturday afternoon. Look for “Confessions” or “Reconciliation” on the parish website or bulletin. You can also call the parish office and ask for an appointment; priests are happy to make time. During Advent and Lent many parishes hold penance services with several priests.

You can go to any parish, not just your own. If you would rather a priest who does not know you, go somewhere else. Find a church near you.

Who can go? Any baptized Catholic. If you are not Catholic, you are welcome to talk with a priest, and many will gladly give you a blessing. People becoming Catholic through OCIA who were already baptized in another church make their first confession before they are received; people who have never been baptized do not need to, because Baptism itself washes away all sin.

How to prepare

Before you go, spend a few quiet minutes looking honestly at your life since your last confession. This is called an examination of conscience. A simple way is to walk through the Ten Commandments and ask yourself questions like these:

  • Have I put God first? Have I prayed? Have I missed Mass on Sundays or holy days without a serious reason?
  • Have I used God’s name carelessly or in anger?
  • Have I honored my parents and cared for my family?
  • Have I hurt anyone by violence, cruelty, hatred or refusing to forgive?
  • Have I been unfaithful, or used pornography or other people sexually?
  • Have I stolen, cheated, or been lazy at work or school?
  • Have I lied, gossiped or damaged someone’s reputation?
  • Have I been envious or greedy?

For serious sins, the Church asks you to name the kind of sin and roughly how many times. You do not need to tell a long story. It is fine to write a short list and bring it in with you.

Step by step

  1. Choose how to confess

    Most churches let you kneel behind a screen and stay anonymous, or sit face to face with the priest. Either is fine.

  2. Begin

    The priest greets you. Make the Sign of the Cross and say: “Bless me, Father, for I have sinned. It has been [how long] since my last confession.” If you are not sure what to say, just tell him, “It’s been a long time and I’m not sure what to do.” He will guide you.

  3. Confess your sins

    Tell him your sins simply. When you are finished, say something like, “For these and all my sins, I am sorry.”

  4. Listen, and receive your penance

    The priest may offer a few words of advice or encouragement. He will give you a penance: usually a few prayers, or an act of kindness, to begin making things right.

  5. Pray an Act of Contrition

    He will ask you to tell God you are sorry. You can say the traditional Act of Contrition (it is fine to read it from your phone or a card), or simply say in your own words that you are sorry and will try to do better.

  6. Receive absolution

    The priest extends his hand and prays the words of absolution, ending, “I absolve you from your sins, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.” Make the Sign of the Cross and answer “Amen.” He may say, “Give thanks to the Lord, for he is good,” and you answer, “His mercy endures for ever.”

  7. Do your penance

    Thank him and go. Do your penance soon, ideally right away in the church. Your sins are forgiven.

If it has been years

Priests hear from people who have been away for 10, 20 or 40 years all the time, and most will tell you it is one of the best parts of their work. You will not be scolded. Say how long it has been, mention the most serious things you remember, and let the priest help with the rest. If you are nervous, call the parish and ask for a time to talk with a priest first. See also Coming back to the Church.

Common questions

Will the priest tell anyone?

No. The seal of confession is absolute. A priest may never reveal anything he hears in confession, to anyone, for any reason, even to save his own life.

What if I forget a sin?

It is forgiven along with the others. If it was serious, simply mention it next time.

How often should I go?

The Church asks Catholics to confess serious sins at least once a year, and before receiving Communion if they are aware of a serious (mortal) sin. Many people go monthly, because it helps.

Why tell a priest instead of just God?

Jesus gave his apostles the power to forgive sins in his name (John 20:21–23), and hearing the words “I absolve you” out loud is a great gift. We answer this more fully in Honest Questions.
