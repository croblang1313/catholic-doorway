How the Catholic Bible is arranged

The Catholic Bible has 73 books: 46 in the Old Testament and 27 in the New. It has seven more Old Testament books than most Protestant Bibles. Catholics call them the deuterocanonical books: Tobit, Judith, 1 and 2 Maccabees, Wisdom, Sirach and Baruch, plus longer versions of Esther and Daniel.

Old Testament (46 books)

The Pentateuch: Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy

Historical books: Joshua, Judges, Ruth, 1 and 2 Samuel, 1 and 2 Kings, 1 and 2 Chronicles, Ezra, Nehemiah, Tobit, Judith, Esther, 1 and 2 Maccabees

Wisdom books: Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs, Wisdom, Sirach

Prophets: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Lamentations, Baruch, Ezekiel, Daniel, and the twelve shorter prophets from Hosea to Malachi

New Testament (27 books)

The Gospels: Matthew, Mark, Luke, John

History of the early Church: Acts of the Apostles

Letters of St. Paul: Romans, 1 and 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, 1 and 2 Thessalonians, 1 and 2 Timothy, Titus, Philemon

Other letters: Hebrews, James, 1 and 2 Peter, 1, 2 and 3 John, Jude

Prophecy: Revelation

New to the Bible? Start here

  1. The Gospel of Mark: the shortest Gospel, fast-moving, and a good first read.
  2. The Gospel of Luke and the Acts of the Apostles: one author’s two-part story of Jesus and the early Church.
  3. The Psalms: the Bible’s prayer book. Try one a day.
  4. Genesis: the beginning of the story of God and his people.

A simple way to pray with Scripture

Catholics have long prayed with the Bible using lectio divina (“divine reading”):

  1. Read a short passage slowly, perhaps today’s Gospel.
  2. Reflect on a word or phrase that stands out to you.
  3. Pray: talk to God about it.
  4. Rest quietly in God’s presence.

How the readings are chosen

The Mass readings follow the Lectionary. Sunday readings follow a three-year cycle (Years A, B and C, focused on Matthew, Mark and Luke, with John read in special seasons). Weekday readings follow a two-year cycle (Years I and II). Over three years of Sundays, Catholics hear most of the Gospels.
