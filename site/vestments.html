What Priests & Deacons Wear

The clothes worn at Mass are called vestments. Each piece has a purpose and a long history, and their color tells you what the Church is celebrating today.

Dress a Priest for Mass

A priest puts his vestments on in this order. Tap each layer, then try the colors of the year.

Layers

Alb. The long white robe worn by priests, deacons and sometimes servers. It recalls the white garment of Baptism; the name comes from the Latin for "white."

Cincture. A cord tied around the waist over the alb, like a belt.

Stole. A long strip of cloth in the color of the day and the sign of ordained ministry. A priest wears it around his neck, hanging straight down in front; a deacon wears it over his left shoulder, crossing to his right hip.

Chasuble. The outermost garment a priest wears at Mass: a large, sleeveless vestment in the liturgical color of the day. It stands for charity and for the "yoke" of Christ.

Who Wears What

Why the Colors Change

The Church year is a cycle that walks through the life of Christ, beginning with Advent. The vestment color is a quick signal of which season or feast it is.

Violet

When: Advent and Lent. It may also be worn at funerals.

Meaning: Waiting, repentance and preparation.

White or Gold

When: Christmas and Easter seasons, Holy Thursday, feasts of the Lord, Mary, the angels and saints who were not martyrs. Often at weddings and funerals.

Meaning: Joy, purity and the glory of the Resurrection.

Red

When: Palm Sunday, Good Friday, Pentecost, Confirmation, and feasts of the Apostles, Evangelists and martyrs.

Meaning: The fire of the Holy Spirit and the blood of those who gave their lives for Christ.

Green

When: Ordinary Time, the longest part of the year, between Christmas and Lent and from Pentecost to Advent.

Meaning: Hope, life and steady growth in faith.

Rose

When: May be worn on two Sundays: the third Sunday of Advent (Gaudete) and the fourth Sunday of Lent (Laetare).

Meaning: Joy breaking through: the waiting is more than half over.

Black

When: May be worn at funerals and on All Souls' Day (November 2).

Meaning: Mourning, and prayer for those who have died.

Vestment by Vestment

Alb
The long white robe worn by priests, deacons and sometimes servers. It recalls the white garment of Baptism; the name comes from the Latin for "white."
Cincture
A cord tied around the waist over the alb, like a belt.
Stole
A long strip of cloth in the color of the day and the sign of ordained ministry. A priest wears it around his neck, hanging straight down in front; a deacon wears it over his left shoulder, crossing to his right hip.
Chasuble
The outermost garment a priest wears at Mass: a large, sleeveless vestment in the liturgical color of the day. It stands for charity and for the "yoke" of Christ.
Dalmatic
The outer vestment of a deacon, with short, wide sleeves, worn over the alb and stole.
Cassock
The long, close-fitting everyday garment of clergy. It is usually black; bishops may wear one with purple trim, cardinals red trim, and the Pope wears white.
Surplice
A loose white garment, shorter than an alb, worn over a cassock, for example by altar servers or by clergy outside of Mass.
Clerical Collar
The white band at the neck of a black shirt or cassock: the everyday sign of a priest or deacon.
Cope
A long, open cape in the liturgical color, worn for processions, Benediction and solemn blessings.
Mitre
The tall, pointed hat a bishop wears during liturgies.
Crozier
The bishop's staff, shaped like a shepherd's crook: a sign that he is the shepherd of his people.
Zucchetto
The small skullcap worn by bishops (violet), cardinals (red) and the Pope (white).
Liturgical Colors
The colors of vestments, which change through the Church year: violet for Advent and Lent; white or gold for Christmas, Easter and feasts of the Lord, Mary and saints who were not martyrs; red for Palm Sunday, Good Friday, Pentecost, the Apostles and martyrs; green for Ordinary Time; and rose or black on a few days.
Vestments
The special garments worn by clergy at Mass. Their color changes with the season.
