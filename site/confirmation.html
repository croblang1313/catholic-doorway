Confirmation

Confirmation completes Baptism with the strength of the Holy Spirit. Most Catholics are confirmed as children or teens, but many adults were baptized and never confirmed. It is never too late, and the path is usually simple.

What Confirmation is

Confirmation is one of the three sacraments of initiation, with Baptism and the Eucharist. At Pentecost the Holy Spirit came upon the apostles and gave them courage to live and share their faith. In Confirmation that same Spirit “seals” the baptized and strengthens them to be Christ’s witnesses in the world.

It is received once in a lifetime. The Church names seven gifts of the Holy Spirit that it strengthens: wisdom, understanding, counsel, fortitude (courage), knowledge, piety and fear of the Lord (wonder and awe before God).

Which path is yours?

A child or teen

Most U.S. dioceses confirm young people between about 7th grade and high school, after one or two years of preparation through the parish or Catholic school. A few dioceses now confirm children around age 7, together with First Communion. Ask your parish what the local age is.

A baptized Catholic adult

Baptized Catholic, maybe received First Communion, but never confirmed? Ask your parish about adult Confirmation. It is usually a short course of a few weeks to a few months, and many dioceses hold an adult Confirmation Mass with the bishop once or twice a year.

Baptized in another Christian church

If you were baptized as a Protestant or Orthodox Christian and want to become Catholic, you are not baptized again. You make a profession of faith and are confirmed and receive Communion, usually at the Easter Vigil after preparation through OCIA.

Not baptized

Adults and children over 7 who are not baptized receive Baptism, Confirmation and First Communion together, usually at the Easter Vigil, after preparing through OCIA.

Step by step

  1. Contact your parish

    Call the parish office or look on its website for “Confirmation,” “youth ministry” or “adult Confirmation.” If you have not registered at the parish, they will usually ask you to.

  2. Get your baptism record

    The parish will need a recent copy of your baptism certificate. If you were baptized somewhere else, here is how to request it.

  3. Prepare

    Classes cover the Creed, the sacraments, prayer and the moral life. Teen programs often include a retreat and service hours. Adult programs are shorter and fit around work.

  4. Choose a sponsor and a name

    See sponsors and names below.

  5. Go to Confession

    Most programs ask candidates to go to Confession shortly before Confirmation, so they receive the sacrament with a clean heart.

  6. Be confirmed

    Usually by the bishop at a special Mass, sometimes by a priest he appoints.

There is no charge for the sacrament itself. Some programs ask a fee for books or a retreat, and parishes help families who cannot pay.

Choosing a sponsor

A sponsor walks with you, prays for you and stands behind you at the ceremony. Church law asks that a sponsor:

  • is at least 16 years old,
  • is a Catholic who has been confirmed and has received the Eucharist,
  • lives a life of faith in keeping with the role (for example, if married, is married in the Church),
  • is not your mother or father.

The Church encourages choosing one of your baptismal godparents, to show how Baptism and Confirmation belong together. Your sponsor may be asked for a sponsor letter from their own parish.

Choosing a Confirmation name

In the U.S. it is customary to choose the name of a saint whose life inspires you, who then becomes a special patron. You can also keep your baptismal name, which the Church especially recommends if it is already a saint’s name. Browse the saints or search the saints directory for ideas.

On the day

Confirmation usually happens at Mass, after the homily:

  1. Candidates renew their baptismal promises, rejecting sin and professing the faith.
  2. The bishop stretches out his hands over everyone and prays for the gifts of the Holy Spirit.
  3. Each candidate comes forward with their sponsor, who places a right hand on the candidate’s shoulder. The bishop anoints the candidate’s forehead with chrism, saying, “Be sealed with the Gift of the Holy Spirit.” The candidate answers, “Amen.”
  4. The bishop says, “Peace be with you,” and the candidate answers, “And with your spirit.”

Dress as you would for a special occasion. Some parishes ask candidates to wear red, the color of the Holy Spirit, or provide red robes.

Common questions

Do I need to be confirmed to get married in the Church?

Church law asks Catholics to be confirmed before marriage if they can do so without great difficulty. Many couples are confirmed during their marriage preparation; ask your parish early.

Do I need to be confirmed to be a godparent?

Yes. A godparent for Baptism, or a sponsor for Confirmation, must be a confirmed Catholic.

I am an adult and nervous about classes with teenagers.

You will not be put in the teen program. Adults prepare separately, often in small groups or one-on-one with a priest, deacon or catechist.

I was confirmed but do not remember it well. Should I do it again?

No. Confirmation leaves a permanent mark and is never repeated. If you are unsure whether you were confirmed, your parish of baptism can check: Confirmation is noted in the margin of your baptism record.
