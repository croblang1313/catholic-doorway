Advent and Christmas

Advent is the four weeks of waiting and hope before Christmas. Then the Church celebrates Christmas not for one day but for a whole season. Here are the dates, the prayers and the traditions, with simple ways to keep them at home.

The Advent calendar

Open one door a day, from the first Sunday of Advent to Christmas Eve. Behind each one is that day’s date, a verse from Scripture, a short reflection and one small thing to do. The doors you open are remembered on this device.

Every day of this Advent

Sunday, November 29 · Day 1 of 26

First Sunday of Advent: hope

Take heed, watch; for you do not know when the time will come.

Mark 13:33

A new Church year begins today. Light the first violet candle on the Advent wreath, the candle of hope. Jesus asks us to stay awake: not anxious, but ready, like a family waiting up for someone they love to come home.

Today: Set up an Advent wreath, or simply light one candle tonight and pray together.

Monday, November 30 · Day 2 of 26

Saint Andrew, the first called

“Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men.” Immediately they left their nets and followed him.

Matthew 4:19-20

Andrew was a fisherman who met Jesus and right away brought his brother Peter to meet him too. Today many Catholics begin the Saint Andrew Christmas prayer, said fifteen times a day until Christmas.

Today: Invite one person to something good: Mass, a meal, a real conversation.

Tuesday, December 1 · Day 3 of 26

Noah and the rainbow

I set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be a sign of the covenant between me and the earth.

Genesis 9:13

After the flood, God promised never to give up on his people, and he hung a rainbow in the sky to say so. Every promise of Advent rests on this one: God keeps his word.

Today: Think of a promise you have made, and keep it today.

Wednesday, December 2 · Day 4 of 26

Count the stars

“Look toward heaven, and number the stars, if you are able to number them.” Then he said to him, “So shall your descendants be.”

Genesis 15:5

Abraham was old and had no children, yet God told him to look up and count the stars. He trusted, and from his family came Jesus. When a hope seems impossible, look up.

Today: Tonight, find a star and tell God the hope you are carrying.

Thursday, December 3 · Day 5 of 26

Saint Francis Xavier

Woe to me if I do not preach the gospel!

1 Corinthians 9:16

Francis Xavier sailed from Europe to India and Japan to share the Gospel, often exhausted and far from home. He is a patron of missionaries, and a reminder that good news is meant to be shared.

Today: Pray for missionaries, or tell someone what your faith means to you.

Friday, December 4 · Day 6 of 26

Ruth, the faithful stranger

Where you go I will go, and where you lodge I will lodge; your people shall be my people, and your God my God.

Ruth 1:16

Ruth was a foreigner who stayed loyal to her widowed mother-in-law, and she became the great-grandmother of King David. Jesus’ family tree has room for outsiders, and so does his Church.

Today: Reach out to someone who is new, alone or far from home.

Saturday, December 5 · Day 7 of 26

David, the shepherd boy

For the LORD sees not as man sees; man looks on the outward appearance, but the LORD looks on the heart.

1 Samuel 16:7

When God sent Samuel to choose a king, he passed over the tall, impressive brothers and chose the youngest, out in the fields with the sheep. Jesus would be born in David’s town and called the Son of David.

Today: Notice someone others overlook, and thank them.

Sunday, December 6 · Day 8 of 26

Second Sunday of Advent: peace

In the wilderness prepare the way of the LORD, make straight in the desert a highway for our God.

Isaiah 40:3

Light two candles today. John the Baptist calls out from the desert: prepare the way of the Lord. Every crooked path in us, every grudge and every habit we keep excusing, is a road God wants to make straight.

Today: Choose one thing to straighten out before Christmas, and start today.

Also today

Saint Nicholas

Nicholas, a bishop in what is now Turkey, is remembered for secret gifts, like gold left at night for a poor family. He is the real saint behind Santa Claus, and children set out their shoes for him.

Monday, December 7 · Day 9 of 26

Saint Ambrose

Thy word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.

Psalm 119:105

Ambrose was the governor of Milan when the people chose him as their bishop, before he was even baptized. His preaching helped lead Saint Augustine to faith. God’s word, heard faithfully, changes lives.

Today: Read tomorrow’s Gospel slowly before bed.

Tuesday, December 8 · Day 10 of 26

The Immaculate Conception

Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you!

Luke 1:28

Today the Church celebrates that Mary, from the first moment of her life, was kept free from sin by God’s grace, to be the mother of his Son. She is the patroness of the United States under this title.

Today: It is a holy day of obligation in the U.S.: go to Mass today, or at a vigil Mass last evening.

Wednesday, December 9 · Day 11 of 26

Saint Juan Diego

He has put down the mighty from their thrones, and exalted those of low degree.

Luke 1:52

Juan Diego was a humble Indigenous man in Mexico to whom Mary appeared in 1531, asking for a church to be built. He worried he was too small for the task. She chose him for exactly that reason.

Today: Pray for someone who feels unimportant, and tell them they matter.

Thursday, December 10 · Day 12 of 26

Our Lady of Loreto: the holy house

And he went down with them and came to Nazareth, and was obedient to them; and his mother kept all these things in her heart.

Luke 2:51

Loreto in Italy honors the house of Nazareth, where Jesus grew up in an ordinary home with Mary and Joseph. God chose to be close to us in kitchens, chores and family life.

Today: Bless your home: walk through it and pray for everyone who lives there.

Friday, December 11 · Day 13 of 26

Comfort, my people

Comfort, comfort my people, says your God.

Isaiah 40:1

These words were first spoken to people in exile who thought God had forgotten them. They are spoken to you too. Advent is God speaking tenderly to tired hearts.

Today: Write a short note of comfort to someone who is grieving or worn out.

Saturday, December 12 · Day 14 of 26

Our Lady of Guadalupe

And a great portent appeared in heaven, a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars.

Revelation 12:1

In 1531 Mary appeared at Tepeyac in Mexico as a young expectant mother, dressed like the people of the land, and left her image on Juan Diego’s cloak. She is the patroness of the Americas and of unborn children.

Today: Pray for mothers expecting a child, and for every child waiting to be born.

Sunday, December 13 · Day 15 of 26

Third Sunday of Advent: rejoice!

Rejoice in the Lord always; again I will say, Rejoice.… The Lord is at hand.

Philippians 4:4-5

This is Gaudete Sunday, from the Latin for “rejoice.” Light the rose candle, and look for rose vestments at Mass. We are more than halfway there, and joy breaks through the waiting because the Lord is near.

Today: Do something just for joy today: sing, visit a friend, laugh, celebrate.

Also today

Saint Lucy

Lucy, whose name means “light,” was a young Christian in Sicily who gave her wealth to the poor and died for her faith around the year 304. In Sweden, girls in white with crowns of candles honor her at dawn.

Monday, December 14 · Day 16 of 26

Saint John of the Cross

God is love, and he who abides in love abides in God, and God abides in him.

1 John 4:16

John was a Spanish Carmelite friar, once imprisoned by his own brothers, who wrote some of the most beautiful poems ever written about God’s love. He taught that in the evening of life we will be judged on love.

Today: Before bed, ask yourself: where did I love well today?

Tuesday, December 15 · Day 17 of 26

Wait for the Lord

Wait for the LORD; be strong, and let your heart take courage; yea, wait for the LORD!

Psalm 27:14

Waiting is hard: in lines, in hospitals, for answers that do not come. Advent turns waiting into prayer. God is not late. He is preparing something, and he is with you while you wait.

Today: The next time you have to wait today, pray instead of scrolling.

Wednesday, December 16 · Day 18 of 26

No room at the inn

She gave birth to her first-born son and wrapped him in swaddling cloths, and laid him in a manger, because there was no place for them in the inn.

Luke 2:7

Tonight Simbang Gabi begins, the Filipino nine days of Masses, and so does Las Posadas, when neighbors walk with Mary and Joseph knocking on doors for shelter. Both ask one question: will I make room for him?

Today: Make room: clear a little time for prayer, or give to a shelter.

Thursday, December 17 · Day 19 of 26

O Wisdom

I came forth from the mouth of the Most High, and covered the earth like a mist.

Sirach 24:3

Today the “O Antiphons” begin: seven ancient titles for Jesus, sung in the last week before Christmas. The first calls him the Wisdom that comes from the mouth of God and orders all things with strength and gentleness.

Today: Sing or read the first verse of “O Come, O Come, Emmanuel.”

Friday, December 18 · Day 20 of 26

O Adonai, Lord

The angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush; and he looked, and lo, the bush was burning, yet it was not consumed.

Exodus 3:2

Adonai means “Lord.” The God who spoke to Moses from the burning bush and set his people free from slavery is the same God who comes at Christmas to set us free.

Today: Pray for anyone you know who is trapped: by addiction, debt or fear.

Saturday, December 19 · Day 21 of 26

O Root of Jesse

There shall come forth a shoot from the stump of Jesse, and a branch shall grow out of his roots.

Isaiah 11:1

Jesse’s family tree looked cut down to a stump, but God made a new shoot grow from it: Jesus. When something in your life seems finished, God can still bring new life.

Today: Pray for your family, living and dead, by name.

Sunday, December 20 · Day 22 of 26

Fourth Sunday of Advent: love

Behold, I am the handmaid of the Lord; let it be to me according to your word.

Luke 1:38

Light all four candles. Today we stand with Mary, a young woman who said yes to God without knowing everything that would follow. Her yes opened the door for Christ to come into the world.

Today: Pray a Hail Mary slowly, and ask for the grace to say yes to God.

Also today

O Key of David

A calendar of doors, and today’s antiphon is about a key! Jesus is the Key of David, who opens what no one can shut. He opens the way to heaven and sets free those who sit in darkness.

Monday, December 21 · Day 23 of 26

O Morning Star

For you who fear my name the sun of righteousness shall rise, with healing in its wings.

Malachi 4:2

In the northern half of the world this is about the darkest day of the year, and on it the Church sings to Jesus, the Dawn, the Sun of justice. From now on the days grow longer, and Christmas is very near.

Today: Watch the sunrise or the sunset, and pray for those who sit in darkness.

Tuesday, December 22 · Day 24 of 26

O King of all nations

For to us a child is born, to us a son is given; and the government will be upon his shoulder, and his name will be called “Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace.”

Isaiah 9:6

Jesus is the King every people longs for, the cornerstone who makes two into one. His rule is not power over people but peace among them.

Today: Make peace with someone, or pray for a part of the world at war.

Wednesday, December 23 · Day 25 of 26

O Emmanuel

Behold, a virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel.

Isaiah 7:14

The last antiphon calls Jesus Emmanuel, “God with us.” Read the first letters of the seven Latin titles backwards and they spell ero cras: “Tomorrow I will be there.”

Today: Make room in your heart before tomorrow: go to Confession if you can.

Thursday, December 24 · Day 26 of 26

Christmas Eve

Be not afraid; for behold, I bring you good news of a great joy which will come to all the people; for to you is born this day in the city of David a Savior, who is Christ the Lord.

Luke 2:10-11

The last door is the great door. Tonight the Church celebrates the birth of Jesus, and every door is open: come as you are. Christmas is not one day but a whole season, so the joy is only beginning.

Today: Go to Christmas Mass tonight or tomorrow, and bring someone with you. Merry Christmas!

What Advent is

Advent (from the Latin adventus, “coming”) begins the Church’s year. It looks two ways at once: back to Christ’s first coming at Bethlehem, and ahead to his coming again at the end of time. Its color is violet, a sign of preparation, with rose on the third Sunday, called Gaudete (“Rejoice!”) Sunday, because Christmas is near.

Advent is quieter than Lent. There is no required fasting, but many Catholics choose a small sacrifice, more prayer, a good Confession and generosity to people in need, so that Christmas finds them ready.

The Advent wreath

An evergreen circle, a sign of eternal life, holds four candles: three violet and one rose. Light one more each week, often at the family dinner table, and say a short prayer together.

  1. Week 1: one violet candle, the candle of hope.
  2. Week 2: two violet candles, peace.
  3. Week 3: add the rose candle, joy.
  4. Week 4: the last violet candle, love.

Some families add a white “Christ candle” in the center on Christmas.

Prayers for each week

Blessing the wreath (first Sunday): Lord our God, bless this wreath and all of us who gather around it. As its light grows each week, may our hearts grow ready to welcome your Son, Jesus Christ, who lives and reigns for ever and ever. Amen.

Week 1: Come, Lord Jesus, and fill us with hope. Help us to stay awake and watch for you. Amen.

Week 2: Come, Lord Jesus, Prince of Peace. Make straight the way of our hearts, and make us peacemakers. Amen.

Week 3: Come, Lord Jesus, our joy. You are near; let us rejoice in you always. Amen.

Week 4: Come, Lord Jesus. With Mary, who said yes to God, help us to love and to welcome you. Amen.

Close with an Our Father and a Hail Mary, or sing “O Come, O Come, Emmanuel.”

The “O Antiphons” (December 17–23)

In the last seven days before Christmas, Evening Prayer and the Gospel verse at Mass call on Jesus by seven names from the prophets, each beginning with “O.” They are the verses of “O Come, O Come, Emmanuel.”

Traditions to keep

Saint Nicholas (December 6)

Children set out their shoes the night before and find small treats in the morning, remembering the generous bishop behind the Santa Claus story.

Our Lady of Guadalupe (December 12)

Patroness of the Americas. Many parishes hold Las Mañanitas, early-morning songs and Mass, often with a procession and a meal afterward.

Simbang Gabi (December 16–24)

A Filipino tradition of nine Masses before Christmas, at dawn or in the evening, honoring Mary and preparing for Jesus’ birth. Parishes with Filipino communities across the U.S. celebrate it, often with bibingka and puto bumbong afterward.

Las Posadas (December 16–24)

A Mexican and Latin American tradition: for nine nights, neighbors walk with Mary and Joseph looking for shelter, sing at doors until they are “let in,” then pray and celebrate together.

The Jesse tree

Each day of Advent, hang an ornament for one part of salvation history, from Creation and Noah to Abraham, David and John the Baptist. A good way to read the Bible’s story with children.

The Nativity scene

Set up the stable early and leave the manger empty. Place the baby Jesus on Christmas Eve, and move the Wise Men closer each day until the Epiphany.

Confession before Christmas

Most parishes add extra Confession times in Advent, and many hold a penance service, an evening of prayer with several priests hearing confessions. Check your parish bulletin or website in early December, because lines grow long in the last week. New to it, or been a while? Our step-by-step guide to going to Confession and the examination of conscience make it easy.

Christmas Mass

Christmas has four Masses, each with its own readings: the Vigil Mass on Christmas Eve afternoon or evening, Mass during the Night (the traditional “Midnight Mass”), Mass at Dawn and Mass during the Day. Any one of them fulfills the obligation.

  • Arrive early. Christmas Eve Masses, especially the early-evening family Mass, are often standing-room only. Some parishes open a second space with a video screen.
  • Everyone is welcome. If you are bringing a friend who is not Catholic, or you are coming yourself after years away, you belong there. Our Your First Mass guide walks you through it on your phone.
  • Communion. Catholics prepared to receive come forward. Anyone else is welcome to come forward with arms crossed over the chest for a blessing, or to stay in the pew and pray.

The Christmas season

Christmas does not end on December 26. The Church keeps an eight-day “octave” of Christmas and a season that runs to the Baptism of the Lord in January. Along the way come the feast of the Holy Family, the solemnity of Mary, Mother of God on January 1, and the Epiphany, when the Wise Men arrive.

On the Epiphany, many families bless their homes by writing over the front door in blessed chalk, for example 20 + C + M + B + 27: the year, joined by the initials of the Wise Men (Caspar, Melchior, Balthasar) and of the Latin blessing Christus mansionem benedicat, “May Christ bless this house.” Ask your parish for blessed chalk.
