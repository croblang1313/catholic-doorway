All the saint stories

In calendar order, by each saint’s feast day. Grown-ups can read more in Meet the Saints.

January 4

St. Elizabeth Ann Seton

Elizabeth was born in New York City in 1774, before the United States was even a country. She was a wife and the mother of five children. After her husband died, she became Catholic, opened a school for girls in Maryland and started a group of sisters to teach children. She is the first saint who was born in the United States.

Try this: Thank God for a teacher who helps you learn, and tell that teacher thank you too.

February 20

Sts. Francisco and Jacinta Marto

Francisco and Jacinta were a brother and sister who looked after sheep in Fátima, Portugal. In 1917, with their cousin Lúcia, they saw Our Lady, who asked them to pray the Rosary for peace. They prayed every day and offered little sacrifices for other people. Both of them got very sick and died young, still trusting Jesus. They are the youngest saints who were not martyrs.

Try this: Pray one decade of the Rosary (an Our Father, ten Hail Marys and a Glory Be) for peace in the world.

March 17

St. Patrick

When Patrick was sixteen, pirates took him from Britain to Ireland, where he had to watch sheep. He was lonely, so he prayed many times every day. After six years he escaped and went home. Later he became a priest and a bishop and went back to Ireland to tell the people about Jesus. People say he used a three-leaf shamrock to explain the Holy Trinity: one God in three Persons.

Try this: Draw a shamrock and write Father, Son and Holy Spirit on its three leaves.

March 19

St. Joseph

Joseph was a carpenter in Nazareth who made things out of wood. God chose him to be Mary’s husband and to take care of Jesus as His father on earth. When an angel spoke to him in dreams, Joseph listened and obeyed right away, even when it meant a long trip to Egypt to keep his family safe. He taught Jesus how to work and how to pray.

Try this: Help with a job at home without being asked, the way Joseph helped his family.

Color St. Joseph →

April 2

St. Pedro Calungsod

Pedro was a teenager from the Philippines. He was a catechist who helped missionaries teach people about Jesus on the island of Guam: he taught prayers, sang and helped at baptisms. In 1672, men who were angry at the missionaries attacked Pedro and Father Diego. Pedro could have run away, but he stayed with the priest. He was about seventeen when he died for his faith.

Try this: Teach someone younger than you how to make the Sign of the Cross.

April 16

St. Bernadette

Bernadette was a poor girl in Lourdes, France, who was often sick. In 1858 a beautiful lady appeared to her in a little cave by the river. The lady asked for prayer, and told Bernadette to dig in the ground, where a spring of water began to flow. The lady said, “I am the Immaculate Conception.” She was Mary! People still come to Lourdes from all over the world to pray.

Try this: Say a Hail Mary for someone who is sick.

May 30

St. Joan of Arc

Joan was a farm girl in France who heard the voices of saints telling her that God had a job for her: to help her country in a war. When she was only seventeen, she led the French army, carrying a banner with the names of Jesus and Mary. She was very brave. Later her enemies captured her and put her to death, but she trusted God to the very end.

Try this: Ask God for courage to do something hard today.

June 13

St. Anthony of Padua

Anthony was a Franciscan friar from Portugal who became a wonderful preacher. He knew the Bible so well that crowds came to hear him. One story says that once, when people would not listen, even the fish came close to the shore to hear him! He loved the poor. Today many people ask Saint Anthony to pray for them when they lose something.

Try this: Next time you lose something, ask Saint Anthony to pray for you, then look carefully!

July 14

St. Kateri Tekakwitha

Kateri was a Mohawk girl born in what is now New York State. Sickness left her with scars on her face and weak eyes. When she was twenty, she was baptized. Some people made fun of her for following Jesus, so she moved to a Christian village in Canada, where she prayed in the woods and made little crosses from sticks. She is the first Native American saint.

Try this: Make a small cross from two sticks tied with string, and keep it where you pray.

August 13

St. Dulce of the Poor

Sister Dulce lived in Salvador, Brazil. She could not bear to see sick and poor people sleeping in the streets, so she brought them in, first to empty houses and then to a chicken yard next to her convent. That chicken yard grew into a big hospital! People called her “the good angel of Bahia.” She was tiny and often sick herself, but she never stopped helping.

Try this: Give some toys or clothes you do not use to children who need them.

August 23

St. Rose of Lima

Rose lived in Lima, Peru. She cared more about loving God than about how she looked. She worked hard sewing and growing flowers to help her family, and she cared for poor and sick people in her own home. She is the first saint born in the Americas.

Try this: Plant a seed or care for a flower, and say a prayer each time you water it.

September 28

St. Lorenzo Ruiz

Lorenzo was a husband and father in Manila, in the Philippines, who helped at his parish church. He sailed to Japan with missionaries at a time when Christians there were in great danger. He was arrested and told to give up his faith, but he said he was a Christian and would give his life for God. He is the first Filipino saint.

Try this: Pray for Christians around the world who are treated unfairly because of their faith.

October 1

St. Thérèse of Lisieux

Thérèse was the youngest of nine children in a loving family in France. At fifteen she became a Carmelite nun. She found a “little way” to be holy: doing small, everyday things with great love, like smiling at someone who bothered her. She died when she was only twenty-four and promised to send a shower of roses from heaven. That is why she is often shown with roses.

Try this: Do one small thing with great love today, without telling anyone.

October 4

St. Francis of Assisi

Francis grew up rich in Assisi, Italy, and loved parties and fancy clothes. Then Jesus changed his heart. He gave away everything, wore a simple brown robe and took care of the poor and the sick. He loved God’s creation and called the sun his brother and the moon his sister. Stories say he even preached to the birds! He started the Franciscans.

Try this: Go outside and thank God for five things you see.

Color St. Francis of Assisi →

October 12

St. Carlo Acutis

Carlo was a boy from Milan, Italy, who loved video games, soccer and computers. He loved Jesus even more and went to Mass every day he could. He used his computer skills to make a website about Eucharistic miracles. He was kind to poor people and stood up for kids who were bullied. He got very sick and died when he was fifteen. In 2025 he was named a saint, and pictures often show him in jeans and sneakers.

Try this: Use some of your screen time for something good, like sending a kind message to a grandparent.

November 3

St. Martin de Porres

Martin was born in Lima, Peru. His father was a Spanish nobleman and his mother was a freed slave from Panama, and some people treated Martin badly because of the color of his skin. He became a Dominican brother and cared for the sick, the poor and even animals: people said that dogs, cats and mice ate peacefully from the same dish when he fed them. He was a friend of Saint Rose.

Try this: Be kind to someone who gets left out.

December 6

St. Nicholas

Nicholas was a bishop long ago in Myra, a town in what is now Turkey. He loved to help people in secret. A famous story says he tossed bags of gold through a window at night so three poor sisters would have what they needed to marry. Because of his secret gifts, children in many countries put out their shoes on the night of December 5 and find small treats in the morning.

Try this: Leave a secret, kind surprise for someone in your family.

Color St. Nicholas →

December 9

St. Juan Diego

Juan Diego was an Indigenous man in Mexico. In December 1531, on a hill called Tepeyac, Mary appeared to him and spoke to him in his own language. She asked for a church to be built there. When the bishop asked for a sign, Mary filled Juan Diego’s cloak, called a tilma, with roses in the middle of winter. When he opened it, her picture was on the cloth: Our Lady of Guadalupe.

Try this: Talk to Mary today in your own words, the way Juan Diego did.
