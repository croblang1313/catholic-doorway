Catholic Terms

Catholic Terms, Explained

Catholics use some words you won’t hear anywhere else. Here they are in plain English. Links across the site point here, so you’re never lost.

A
Absolution

The words of forgiveness a priest speaks in Confession, through which God forgives sins.

Related Terms: Confession, Confessional, Examination of Conscience

Adoration

Prayer before the Eucharist, often displayed on the altar in a monstrance. Many parishes have hours set aside for it.

Related Terms: Blessed Sacrament, Cope, Eucharist, Monstrance, Real Presence

Advent

The four weeks before Christmas, a season of hopeful preparation for Christ's coming. Its color is violet.

Related Terms: Lent, Liturgical Colors, Ordinary Time

Alb

The long white robe worn by priests, deacons and sometimes servers. It recalls the white garment of Baptism; the name comes from the Latin for "white."

Related Terms: Chasuble, Cincture, Dalmatic, Stole

See it in the vestments guide →

Altar

The table at the center of the sanctuary where the Eucharist is celebrated. It represents Christ himself.

Related Terms: Chalice, Crucifix, Eucharist, Sanctuary, Tabernacle

See it on the church map →

Altar Server

Someone, often a young parishioner, who assists the priest at Mass: carrying the cross and candles, holding the book and bringing up the gifts.

Related Terms: Alb, Altar, Surplice

Ambo

The reading stand in the sanctuary from which the Scripture readings, the Psalm and the Gospel are proclaimed. Also called the lectern or pulpit.

Related Terms: Homily, Lectionary, Sanctuary

See it on the church map →

Amen

Hebrew for "truly" or "so be it." Said at the end of a prayer to make it your own. At Communion it means "Yes, I believe this is Christ."

Related Terms: Creed, Eucharistic Prayer

Annulment

Formally a "declaration of nullity": a finding by a Church tribunal that a marriage was missing something essential from the start, so a valid sacramental bond never formed. It is not a Catholic divorce.

Related Terms: Diocese, Sacrament

Apostolic Succession

The unbroken line of bishops, each ordained by earlier bishops, going back to the Apostles.

Related Terms: Cathedral, Diocese, Magisterium, Vatican

Assumption

The belief that Mary, at the end of her earthly life, was taken body and soul into heaven. Celebrated August 15.

Related Terms: Holy Day of Obligation, Immaculate Conception

B
Baptismal Font

The basin or pool where Baptisms take place. Many churches place it near the entrance, because Baptism is the doorway into the Church.

Related Terms: Holy Water, Narthex, Original Sin

See it on the church map →

Beatification

The step at which the Church declares a person "Blessed." See the Saints page.

Related Terms: Canonization, Communion of Saints

Blessed Sacrament

The Eucharist, especially the consecrated hosts kept in the tabernacle.

Related Terms: Adoration, Eucharist, Monstrance, Sanctuary Lamp, Tabernacle

C
Candidate

In OCIA, someone already baptized in another Christian church who is preparing to become Catholic.

Related Terms: Catechumen, Inquirer, OCIA, Sponsor

Canonization

The Pope's declaration that a person is a saint in heaven and may be honored by the whole Church.

Related Terms: Beatification, Communion of Saints, Doctor of the Church

Cassock

The long, close-fitting everyday garment of clergy. It is usually black; bishops may wear one with purple trim, cardinals red trim, and the Pope wears white.

Related Terms: Clerical Collar, Surplice, Zucchetto

See it in the vestments guide →

Catechism

A summary of the Church's teaching. The Catechism of the Catholic Church (1992) is the main reference today.

Related Terms: Creed, Magisterium

Catechumen

An unbaptized person in OCIA who has celebrated the Rite of Acceptance and is preparing for Baptism.

Related Terms: Candidate, Elect, Inquirer, OCIA

Cathedral

The bishop's own church, the mother church of a diocese. It holds the bishop's chair, called the cathedra.

Related Terms: Diocese, Mitre, Presider's Chair

Chalice

The cup that holds the wine that becomes the Blood of Christ at Mass.

Related Terms: Altar, Ciborium, Paten

See it on the church map →

Chasuble

The outermost garment a priest wears at Mass: a large, sleeveless vestment in the liturgical color of the day. It stands for charity and for the "yoke" of Christ.

Related Terms: Alb, Liturgical Colors, Stole

See it in the vestments guide →

Chrism

Holy oil blessed by the bishop, used at Baptism, Confirmation and Holy Orders.

Related Terms: Confirmation, Sacrament, Sacramental

Ciborium

A covered, cup-shaped vessel that holds the consecrated hosts for Communion and for keeping in the tabernacle.

Related Terms: Chalice, Eucharist, Tabernacle

See it on the church map →

Cincture

A cord tied around the waist over the alb, like a belt.

Related Terms: Alb

See it in the vestments guide →

Clerical Collar

The white band at the neck of a black shirt or cassock: the everyday sign of a priest or deacon.

Related Terms: Cassock, Deacon

See it in the vestments guide →

Communion of Saints

The bond uniting all members of the Church: those on earth, in purgatory and in heaven.

Related Terms: Canonization, Purgatory

Conclave

The closed meeting in which cardinals under age 80 elect a new Pope. White smoke means a Pope has been chosen.

Related Terms: Vatican, Zucchetto

Confession

The sacrament of Penance, or Reconciliation, in which a priest grants God's forgiveness for sins.

Related Terms: Absolution, Confessional, Examination of Conscience, Mortal Sin, Venial Sin

Confessional

The room or booth where Confession is heard. Most let you choose to kneel behind a screen or sit face to face with the priest.

Related Terms: Absolution, Confession

See it on the church map →

Confirmation

The sacrament that strengthens the baptized with the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

Related Terms: Chrism, Pentecost, Sponsor

Consecration

The moment at Mass when the priest says Jesus' words, "This is my Body ... This is the chalice of my Blood," and the bread and wine become Christ's Body and Blood.

Related Terms: Eucharist, Eucharistic Prayer, Real Presence, Transubstantiation

Cope

A long, open cape in the liturgical color, worn for processions, Benediction and solemn blessings.

Related Terms: Adoration, Liturgical Colors, Monstrance

See it in the vestments guide →

Creed

A statement of belief. Catholics pray the Nicene Creed at Sunday Mass and the Apostles' Creed in the Rosary.

Related Terms: Catechism, Incarnation, Trinity

Crozier

The bishop's staff, shaped like a shepherd's crook: a sign that he is the shepherd of his people.

Related Terms: Diocese, Mitre

See it in the vestments guide →

Crucifix

A cross bearing the figure of Jesus. One is always on or near the altar at Mass, recalling that the Mass makes present his sacrifice on the cross.

Related Terms: Altar, Stations of the Cross

See it on the church map →

D
Dalmatic

The outer vestment of a deacon, with short, wide sleeves, worn over the alb and stole.

Related Terms: Alb, Deacon, Stole

See it in the vestments guide →

Deacon

An ordained minister who proclaims the Gospel, preaches, baptizes, witnesses marriages and serves the poor. Permanent deacons may be married.

Related Terms: Dalmatic, Homily, Parish, Stole

Deuterocanonical Books

Seven Old Testament books (Tobit, Judith, 1 and 2 Maccabees, Wisdom, Sirach and Baruch) plus parts of Esther and Daniel, found in Catholic Bibles but not most Protestant ones.

Related Terms: Lectionary, Magisterium

Diocese

A local church in a defined area, led by a bishop. A large or historic one may be an archdiocese.

Related Terms: Apostolic Succession, Cathedral, Eparchy, Parish

Doctor of the Church

A title given to saints whose teaching has especially enriched the whole Church, such as Augustine, Aquinas and Thérèse of Lisieux.

Related Terms: Canonization

E
Elect

In OCIA, catechumens after the Rite of Election on the First Sunday of Lent, now preparing for the sacraments at Easter.

Related Terms: Catechumen, Scrutinies, Triduum

Encyclical

A teaching letter from the Pope to the whole Church.

Related Terms: Magisterium, Vatican

Eparchy

The Eastern Catholic name for a diocese. It is led by an eparch (bishop).

Related Terms: Diocese

Eucharist

From the Greek for "thanksgiving." The sacrament of Christ's Body and Blood, and another name for the Mass.

Related Terms: Blessed Sacrament, Ciborium, Consecration, Monstrance, Real Presence, Tabernacle, Transubstantiation

Eucharistic Prayer

The central prayer of the Mass, in which the priest asks the Holy Spirit to come upon the gifts and repeats Jesus' words at the Last Supper, and the bread and wine become Christ's Body and Blood.

Related Terms: Amen, Consecration, Transubstantiation

Examination of Conscience

Prayerful reflection on one's thoughts, words and actions, usually before Confession.

Related Terms: Confession, Mortal Sin, Venial Sin

G
Genuflect

To bend the right knee to the floor as a sign of reverence toward the tabernacle.

Related Terms: Kneeler, Real Presence, Sign of the Cross, Tabernacle

Grace

God's free gift of his own life and help, given to make us holy.

Related Terms: Original Sin, Sacrament

H
Holy Day of Obligation

A feast on which Catholics are asked to attend Mass, like a Sunday. In the U.S. these include Christmas and the Immaculate Conception (December 8).

Related Terms: Assumption, Immaculate Conception, Mass

Holy Water

Water blessed by a priest. Catholics bless themselves with it on entering church as a reminder of Baptism.

Related Terms: Baptismal Font, Narthex, Sacramental, Sign of the Cross

See it on the church map →

Homily

The reflection on the Scripture readings given by a priest or deacon at Mass.

Related Terms: Ambo, Deacon, Lectionary

I
Immaculate Conception

The belief that Mary was preserved from original sin from the first moment of her conception. It is not the same as the virgin birth of Jesus. Celebrated December 8.

Related Terms: Assumption, Holy Day of Obligation, Original Sin

Incarnation

The Son of God becoming man in Jesus Christ.

Related Terms: Creed, Trinity

Indulgence

A remission, granted through the Church, of the temporal punishment still due for sins that have already been forgiven. It is often attached to a prayer or pilgrimage.

Related Terms: Communion of Saints, Confession, Purgatory

Inquirer

Someone in the first stage of OCIA, exploring the Catholic faith with no commitment.

Related Terms: Candidate, Catechumen, OCIA

K
Kneeler

The padded bar in front of a pew, folded down for kneeling during parts of the Mass.

Related Terms: Genuflect, Pew

See it on the church map →

L
Lectio Divina

Latin for "divine reading": a slow, prayerful way of reading Scripture: read, reflect, pray and rest.

Related Terms: Lectionary, Liturgy of the Hours

Lectionary

The book of Scripture readings used at Mass, arranged by day and season.

Related Terms: Ambo, Homily, Missal

Lent

The 40 days before Easter, beginning on Ash Wednesday, a season of prayer, fasting and almsgiving. Its color is violet.

Related Terms: Liturgical Colors, Scrutinies, Stations of the Cross, Triduum

Liturgical Colors

The colors of vestments, which change through the Church year: violet for Advent and Lent; white or gold for Christmas, Easter and feasts of the Lord, Mary and saints who were not martyrs; red for Palm Sunday, Good Friday, Pentecost, the Apostles and martyrs; green for Ordinary Time; and rose or black on a few days.

Related Terms: Advent, Chasuble, Lent, Ordinary Time, Pentecost

See it in the vestments guide →

Liturgy

The Church's public worship, above all the Mass and the sacraments.

Related Terms: Liturgical Colors, Liturgy of the Hours, Mass

Liturgy of the Hours

The Church's daily prayer of psalms and readings at set times, including Morning Prayer and Evening Prayer (Vespers). Also called the Divine Office or Breviary.

Related Terms: Lectio Divina, Liturgy

M
Magisterium

The teaching office of the Church: the Pope and the bishops in communion with him.

Related Terms: Apostolic Succession, Catechism, Encyclical

Mass

The central act of Catholic worship, in which the Word of God is proclaimed and the Eucharist is celebrated.

Related Terms: Eucharist, Eucharistic Prayer, Homily, Liturgy, Missal

Missal

The book of prayers for Mass. Pew missals help the congregation follow along.

Related Terms: Lectionary, Liturgy

Mitre

The tall, pointed hat a bishop wears during liturgies.

Related Terms: Cathedral, Crozier, Zucchetto

See it in the vestments guide →

Monstrance

An ornate stand, often shaped like a sunburst, used to display the Eucharist for adoration.

Related Terms: Adoration, Blessed Sacrament, Cope, Eucharist

See it on the church map →

Mortal Sin

A grave sin committed with full knowledge and deliberate consent, which breaks one's relationship with God until repented and confessed.

Related Terms: Confession, Grace, Venial Sin

Mystagogy

The final period of OCIA, from Easter to Pentecost, when new Catholics reflect on the sacraments they received.

Related Terms: Neophyte, OCIA

N
Narthex

The entrance hall or lobby of a church, between the outside doors and the nave. Holy water, bulletins and notices are usually here.

Related Terms: Baptismal Font, Holy Water, Nave

See it on the church map →

Nave

The main body of the church, where the congregation sits, usually in pews. The name comes from the Latin word for "ship."

Related Terms: Narthex, Pew, Sanctuary

See it on the church map →

Neophyte

A newly baptized Catholic, especially during the first year after Easter.

Related Terms: Elect, Mystagogy

Novena

A prayer repeated for nine days in a row for a particular intention.

Related Terms: Pentecost, Rosary, Votive Candles

O
OCIA

The Order of Christian Initiation of Adults (formerly RCIA): the process by which adults become Catholic. See Begin Here.

Related Terms: Candidate, Catechumen, Elect, Inquirer, Mystagogy, Scrutinies, Sponsor

Ordinary Time

The weeks of the Church year outside the major seasons. "Ordinary" comes from "ordinal," meaning counted weeks. Its color is green.

Related Terms: Advent, Lent, Liturgical Colors

Original Sin

The wounded human condition inherited from the first sin of our first parents, washed away in Baptism.

Related Terms: Baptismal Font, Grace, Immaculate Conception

P
Parish

A local community of Catholics within a diocese, led by a pastor.

Related Terms: Deacon, Diocese, Pastor

Pastor

The priest appointed by the bishop to lead a parish.

Related Terms: Diocese, Parish

Paten

The small plate, usually of gold or silver, that holds the large host during Mass.

Related Terms: Altar, Chalice

See it on the church map →

Pentecost

The feast 50 days after Easter celebrating the Holy Spirit's coming upon the Apostles, often called the birthday of the Church. Its color is red.

Related Terms: Confirmation, Liturgical Colors, Novena

Pew

A long bench where the congregation sits. Most Catholic pews have a fold-down kneeler and a rack for hymnals and missals.

Related Terms: Kneeler, Nave

See it on the church map →

Presider's Chair

The chair in the sanctuary where the priest sits and from which he leads the opening and closing parts of Mass, a sign of his role as the one who leads the prayer.

Related Terms: Altar, Cathedral, Sanctuary

See it on the church map →

Purgatory

A final purification after death for those who die in God's friendship but are not yet fully purified, so they can enter heaven.

Related Terms: Communion of Saints, Indulgence

R
Real Presence

The belief that Jesus Christ is truly and fully present, Body, Blood, soul and divinity, in the Eucharist.

Related Terms: Eucharist, Genuflect, Monstrance, Tabernacle, Transubstantiation

Rosary

A prayer with beads, meditating on events in the lives of Jesus and Mary. See the Prayers page.

Related Terms: Novena, Sacramental

S
Sacrament

A visible sign instituted by Christ that gives grace. There are seven.

Related Terms: Confession, Confirmation, Eucharist, Grace, Sacramental

Sacramental

A blessed object or action, such as holy water, a rosary or a blessing, that helps us pray and draws us toward the sacraments.

Related Terms: Holy Water, Rosary, Sacrament, Votive Candles

Sanctuary

The area around the altar, often raised a few steps, where the priest and ministers lead the Mass. It holds the altar, the ambo and the presider's chair.

Related Terms: Altar, Ambo, Presider's Chair, Sanctuary Lamp, Tabernacle

See it on the church map →

Sanctuary Lamp

A candle, usually in red glass, that burns near the tabernacle day and night. When it is lit, the Blessed Sacrament is present there.

Related Terms: Blessed Sacrament, Tabernacle

See it on the church map →

Scrutinies

Rites of prayer and healing for the elect on the Third, Fourth and Fifth Sundays of Lent.

Related Terms: Elect, Lent

Sign of the Cross

Touching your forehead, chest, left shoulder and right shoulder while saying "In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen." Catholics begin and end prayers with it.

Related Terms: Genuflect, Holy Water, Trinity

Sponsor

A practicing Catholic who accompanies someone through OCIA or Confirmation.

Related Terms: Candidate, Catechumen, Confirmation

Stations of the Cross

Fourteen images around the walls of a church that show Jesus' path to the cross. People walk and pray them, especially on Fridays in Lent.

Related Terms: Crucifix, Lent

See it on the church map →

Stole

A long strip of cloth in the color of the day and the sign of ordained ministry. A priest wears it around his neck, hanging straight down in front; a deacon wears it over his left shoulder, crossing to his right hip.

Related Terms: Chasuble, Dalmatic, Deacon, Liturgical Colors

See it in the vestments guide →

Surplice

A loose white garment, shorter than an alb, worn over a cassock, for example by altar servers or by clergy outside of Mass.

Related Terms: Alb, Altar Server, Cassock

See it in the vestments guide →

T
Tabernacle

The locked box, often gold and marked by a burning candle nearby, where the Eucharist is kept in a church.

Related Terms: Blessed Sacrament, Ciborium, Eucharist, Genuflect, Real Presence, Sanctuary Lamp

See it on the church map →

Transubstantiation

The Church's term for the change of the whole substance of bread and wine into Christ's Body and Blood, while their appearance remains.

Related Terms: Consecration, Eucharist, Real Presence

Triduum

The three holiest days of the year: Holy Thursday evening through Easter Sunday evening.

Related Terms: Elect, Lent, Liturgical Colors

Trinity

One God in three Persons: the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit.

Related Terms: Creed, Incarnation, Sign of the Cross

V
Vatican

Vatican City, the world's smallest independent state, home of the Pope. Also shorthand for the Church's central government.

Related Terms: Conclave, Encyclical

Venial Sin

A less serious sin that weakens but does not break one's relationship with God.

Related Terms: Confession, Mortal Sin

Vestments

The special garments worn by clergy at Mass. Their color changes with the season.

Related Terms: Alb, Chasuble, Dalmatic, Liturgical Colors, Stole

See it in the vestments guide →

Votive Candles

Small candles that people light before a statue or icon, as a sign of a prayer they leave behind. A small offering is customary but never required.

Related Terms: Novena, Sacramental

See it on the church map →

Z
Zucchetto

The small skullcap worn by bishops (violet), cardinals (red) and the Pope (white).

Related Terms: Cassock, Conclave, Mitre

See it in the vestments guide →
