Saints

The Communion of Saints

Saints are ordinary people who let God make them extraordinary: fishermen and scholars, mothers and soldiers, teenagers and popes. Catholics ask for their prayers and look to their example.

The path to sainthood

Anyone in heaven is a saint. When the Church canonizes someone, it declares with certainty that they are in heaven and may be honored by the whole Church. The process usually begins at least five years after a person’s death and has four steps.

Step 1Servant of GodThe local bishop opens a cause and investigates the person’s life and writings.
Step 2VenerableThe Pope declares that the person lived the virtues to a heroic degree.
Step 3BlessedBeatification follows one approved miracle through their prayers, or martyrdom.
Step 4SaintCanonization usually follows a second approved miracle.

Causes are overseen by the Vatican’s Dicastery for the Causes of Saints. Statuses on this page were reviewed in November 2026 and may change as causes advance.

Meet the saints

Saint

St. Joseph

1st century · Feast: March 19

Husband of Mary and foster father of Jesus. A quiet, faithful carpenter who protected the Holy Family.

Patron of the universal Church, fathers, workers
Saint

St. Peter

d. c. 64 · Feast: June 29

A fisherman Jesus called to be the "rock" of his Church. Leader of the Apostles and the first Bishop of Rome, martyred in Rome.

Apostle, first pope
Saint

St. Paul

d. c. 64-67 · Feast: June 29

Once a persecutor of Christians, he met the risen Christ and became the great missionary to the Gentiles. His letters fill much of the New Testament.

Apostle, missionary
Saint

St. Patrick

5th century · Feast: March 17

Kidnapped as a teenager and enslaved in Ireland, he escaped, became a priest and returned to bring the Gospel to the Irish people.

Ireland, missionary
Saint

St. Augustine of Hippo

354-430 · Feast: August 28

After a restless youth, he was converted through the prayers of his mother, St. Monica. His Confessions is one of the most-read books in history.

Doctor of the Church, bishop, convert
Saint

St. Benedict of Nursia

c. 480-547 · Feast: July 11

Wrote the Rule that shaped Western monastic life around prayer and work: ora et labora.

Monks, patron of Europe
Saint

St. Francis of Assisi

1181/82-1226 · Feast: October 4

A wealthy young man who gave up everything to follow Christ in poverty. Founder of the Franciscans and lover of all God's creatures. Canonized 1228.

Poverty, creation, peace
Saint

St. Clare of Assisi

1194-1253 · Feast: August 11

Friend and follower of St. Francis who founded the Poor Clares, a community of contemplative sisters.

Contemplative, founder
Saint

St. Thomas Aquinas

1225-1274 · Feast: January 28

Dominican friar and the Church's great theologian, author of the Summa Theologiae. Canonized 1323.

Doctor of the Church, students, theologian
Saint

St. Catherine of Siena

1347-1380 · Feast: April 29

A laywoman and mystic who cared for the sick and boldly urged the Pope to return to Rome. Canonized 1461.

Doctor of the Church, mystic, patron of Europe
Saint

St. Joan of Arc

1412-1431 · Feast: May 30

A peasant girl who, guided by heavenly voices, led the French army before being burned at the stake at 19. Canonized 1920.

France, soldiers, martyr
Saint

Jesuits, spiritual exercises

Common questions

Do Catholics worship saints?

No. Worship belongs to God alone. Catholics honor the saints and ask for their prayers, the way you might ask a friend to pray for you.

What is a feast day?

The day the Church remembers a saint, usually the day they died, their “birthday into heaven.” Some feasts on this page follow the U.S. calendar.

What is a patron saint?

A saint associated with a place, profession, cause or need, often because of their own life story. St. Thomas Aquinas, a great scholar, is a patron of students.
