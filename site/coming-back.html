Coming back to the Church

Whether you drifted away after Confirmation, left angry, or just got busy, the door is open. There is no waiting period, no penalty and no form to fill out. Here is how people usually find their way back.

  1. Just go to Mass

    Pick a parish and go on a Sunday (or Saturday evening). Sit wherever you like. You may notice a few changes since you were last there: in 2011 some responses changed, for example the people now answer “And with your spirit.” Our Mass guide shows every response. Find a church near you.

  2. Talk with a priest

    Call the parish office and say, “I’ve been away from the Church and I’d like to talk with a priest about coming back.” That is a very ordinary request. Some parishes also run small groups for returning Catholics, sometimes called Landings or Catholics Returning Home.

  3. Go to Confession

    Confession is the way home. If you have been away from Mass for a long time, the Church asks you to go to Confession before receiving Communion again. Our Confession guide walks you through it, including what to say when it has been years.

  4. Receive Communion

    After Confession, you are free to receive the Eucharist again. Many people say this is the moment they really feel home.

  5. Join a parish

    Register so the parish knows you, and look for one way to get involved: a Bible study, a men’s or women’s group, or serving others. See How to join a parish.

Special situations

I was married outside the Church

You can and should come back to Mass. If you are a Catholic who married outside the Church without permission, the marriage can often be recognized by the Church through a simple process called convalidation. If either of you was married before, the earlier marriage will need to be looked at first. Until things are sorted out you would not receive Communion, but you belong at Mass, and a priest can help you start. See special marriage situations.

I was never confirmed

Ask your parish about Confirmation for adults. It is usually a short preparation, often in a small group, with Confirmation by the bishop once a year.

I left because I was hurt

Many people were. Say so to the priest; it matters, and you deserve to be heard. If one parish did not feel like home, try another.

I’m not sure I believe everything

Come anyway, and bring your questions. Our Honest Questions and What Catholics believe pages are good places to start.
