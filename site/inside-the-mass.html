Inside the Mass

Catholics stand, sit, kneel and answer the priest at set moments. None of it is random: each posture and response says something with the body and voice. Here is the why behind each part.

Why We Stand, Sit and Kneel

Standing

Standing is the posture of respect and readiness, like rising when someone important enters. Early Christians prayed standing as people of the Resurrection. We stand for the Gospel, because Christ himself speaks, and for the great prayers.

Sitting

Sitting is the posture of listening and taking things in, like Mary of Bethany sitting at Jesus' feet (Luke 10:39). We sit for the first readings, the homily and while the gifts are prepared.

Kneeling

Kneeling is the posture of adoration and humility. We kneel when the bread and wine become the Body and Blood of Christ and he is present on the altar, as people knelt before Jesus in the Gospels.

Customs vary a little by country and parish. In the United States the bishops ask the faithful to kneel from after the Holy, Holy, Holy until after the Great Amen, and again after the Lamb of God. If kneeling is hard for you, sitting is always fine.

Gestures and What They Mean

Sign of the Cross

At the start and end of Mass and at the blessing.

It sums up the faith: the Trinity, and the cross by which we are saved.

Three small crosses

As the Gospel is announced, on forehead, lips and heart.

A silent prayer: may God's word be in my mind, on my lips and in my heart.

Striking the breast

At "through my fault" in the I Confess.

A sign of sorrow, like the tax collector who beat his breast and prayed, "God, be merciful to me, a sinner" (Luke 18:13).

A bow

In the Creed at the words about Jesus becoming man, and before receiving Communion.

Reverence for the mystery of God becoming one of us, and for Christ in the Eucharist.

Genuflecting

Before entering the pew and when passing the tabernacle.

Bending the knee to Christ present in the tabernacle (Philippians 2:10).

Sign of peace

Just before Communion.

Jesus asks us to be reconciled before bringing our gift to the altar (Matthew 5:23–24).

Why We Respond

The Mass is a conversation. The priest speaks for the Church to God and to the people, and the people answer. Most responses come straight from the Bible.

Priest
The Lord be with you.
People
And with your spirit.

An ancient greeting found in St. Paul's letters (2 Timothy 4:22). The people pray for the priest and recognize the Holy Spirit he received at ordination.

Priest
The word of the Lord.
People
Thanks be to God.

Thanks for God speaking to us in the reading just heard.

Priest
The Gospel of the Lord.
People
Praise to you, Lord Jesus Christ.

Spoken to Christ himself, who is present in the Gospel.

Priest
Lift up your hearts.
People
We lift them up to the Lord.

An invitation used since the earliest centuries: set aside distractions for the great prayer of thanksgiving.

Priest
Holy, Holy, Holy Lord God of hosts…

The song of the angels in Isaiah 6:3 and Revelation 4:8, joined to the crowd's "Hosanna" on Palm Sunday (Matthew 21:9). Heaven and earth sing together.

Priest
Behold the Lamb of God…
People
Lord, I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof, but only say the word and my soul shall be healed.

The words of the Roman centurion who trusted Jesus to heal his servant from afar (Matthew 8:8).

Priest
The Body of Christ.
People
Amen.

"Yes, I believe it." Each communicant makes a personal act of faith that this is truly Christ.
