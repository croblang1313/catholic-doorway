How to join a parish

In the United States, Catholics usually register at a parish. It is free, it takes about ten minutes, and it makes many things easier later on.

Why register?

Church law says you belong to the parish where you live, registered or not. But parishes keep a list of members, and being on it helps when you need the parish:

  • Being a godparent or Confirmation sponsor. You will usually need a letter from your parish saying you are a registered, practicing Catholic.
  • Baptisms and weddings. Parishes schedule these for their members first.
  • Catholic school. Many schools offer lower tuition to registered, active parishioners.
  • Simply being known. The parish can reach you, and you will hear about events, groups and ways to help.

How to do it

  1. Choose a parish

    Most people join the parish nearest their home, but in the U.S. you can usually join any parish you like, perhaps for its Mass times, its school or its community. Find churches near you.

  2. Find the registration form

    Look on the parish website for “Register,” “Join” or “New parishioners.” Many have an online form. If not, pick up a form in the back of church, or call or visit the parish office.

  3. Fill it in

    Expect to give names, address, phone and email for everyone in your household, and which sacraments each person has received (Baptism, First Communion, Confirmation, marriage). If you know where you were baptized, include it.

  4. Say hello

    Introduce yourself to the priest after Mass. Some parishes hold a welcome night or send a welcome packet. You may be offered offertory envelopes or online giving; giving is encouraged but never required to belong.

Good to know

  • Moving? Register at your new parish. It is a kindness to let your old parish know, but not required.
  • Not Catholic yet? You are welcome to come to Mass and parish events anytime. To become Catholic, ask about OCIA.
  • Need a sponsor letter? Ask well in advance. Some parishes want to see you at Mass regularly for a few months before they write one.
