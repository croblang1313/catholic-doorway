Your first Mass, step by step

Never been to Mass, or it’s been a long time? This guide walks you through all 30 moments of a Sunday Mass: when to stand, sit and kneel, what people say, and what to do at Communion.

In the pew

Use it during Mass

Opens one step at a time in large print with big Back and Next buttons, so you can follow along on your phone. Your screen stays on and your place is saved.

  • Stand
  • Sit
  • Kneel
  • As you wish

Customs vary a little from parish to parish. When in doubt, follow the people around you. If kneeling is hard for you, sitting is always fine. The words people say come from the Roman Missal; the full texts are in the missal or worship aid in the pew.

Before Mass

As you wish

1. Arriving

Arrive ten minutes early and silence your phone. Many Catholics dip their fingers in the holy water at the door and make the Sign of the Cross, then bow or briefly kneel on the right knee (genuflect) toward the tabernacle before entering a pew.

None of this is required of visitors. Sitting quietly and taking it in is perfectly fine. Many people kneel and pray silently before Mass begins.

Introductory Rites

Stand

2. Entrance

Everyone stands as the procession comes up the aisle: a cross, servers, readers, sometimes a deacon carrying the Book of the Gospels, and finally the priest. A hymn is usually sung.

Hymn numbers are often posted on a board at the front, or printed in a worship aid.

Stand

3. Sign of the Cross and greeting

The priest begins with the Sign of the Cross, and everyone makes it with him: touch your forehead, chest, left shoulder, then right shoulder. Then he greets the people.

Priest
In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.
All
Amen.
Priest
The Lord be with you.
All
And with your spirit.

“And with your spirit” is the response you will hear most often during Mass.

Stand

4. Asking for mercy

Everyone pauses to call to mind their sins and ask for God’s mercy. Often the people pray a short prayer of confession together (the “I confess”), which is printed in the missal.

Priest or cantor
Lord, have mercy.
All
Lord, have mercy.
Christ, have mercy.
All
Christ, have mercy.
Lord, have mercy.
All
Lord, have mercy.

Sometimes this is sung in Greek: “Kyrie, eleison” and “Christe, eleison.”

Stand

5. Glory to God

On Sundays (except during Advent and Lent) and on feast days, the congregation sings or says the ancient hymn “Glory to God in the highest.”

The words are in the missal or worship aid. It’s fine just to listen.

Stand

6. Opening prayer

The priest says “Let us pray,” pauses for a moment of silence, then prays the day’s opening prayer (the Collect).

All
Amen.

“Amen” means “yes, so be it.” It’s how the people make the priest’s prayer their own.

Liturgy of the Word

Sit

7. First reading

Everyone sits. A reader proclaims a passage, usually from the Old Testament (from the Acts of the Apostles during the Easter season).

Reader
The word of the Lord.
All
Thanks be to God.

Today’s readings are on our Bible & Readings page if you’d like to read along afterward.

Sit

8. Responsorial Psalm

A cantor sings a psalm. The people repeat a short refrain after each verse.

All
Repeat the refrain the cantor sings first.

The refrain is usually printed in the worship aid or shown on a screen.

Sit

9. Second reading

On Sundays and major feasts there is a second reading, usually from one of the New Testament letters.

Reader
The word of the Lord.
All
Thanks be to God.

Weekday Masses skip this reading, so they are shorter, about 30 minutes.

Stand

10. Gospel acclamation

Everyone stands to welcome the Gospel by singing “Alleluia.” During Lent, another acclamation replaces the Alleluia.

All
Alleluia, alleluia, alleluia.

Standing for the Gospel shows special honor to the words and deeds of Jesus.

Stand

11. The Gospel

The priest or deacon reads from one of the four Gospels. As he announces it, Catholics trace a small cross with the thumb on the forehead, lips and heart, asking that God’s word be in their minds, on their lips and in their hearts.

Priest
The Lord be with you.
All
And with your spirit.
Priest
A reading from the holy Gospel according to…
All
Glory to you, O Lord.
After the reading
The Gospel of the Lord.
All
Praise to you, Lord Jesus Christ.
Sit

12. Homily

Everyone sits while the priest or deacon preaches, explaining the readings and how to live them.

Homilies usually last 5 to 15 minutes.

Stand

13. The Creed

On Sundays and solemnities, everyone stands and professes the faith together, usually with the Nicene Creed. At the words about Jesus becoming man (“and by the Holy Spirit was incarnate…”), everyone bows.

All
The words are in the missal or worship aid.

On Christmas and the Annunciation, people kneel at those words instead of bowing.

Stand

14. Prayers of the Faithful

A deacon or reader offers short prayers for the Church, the world, people in need, the sick and the dead.

Reader
Let us pray to the Lord.
All
Lord, hear our prayer.

The response is sometimes different. Listen to the first one and join in.

Liturgy of the Eucharist

Sit

15. Preparing the gifts

Everyone sits. Bread and wine are brought to the altar, often carried up the aisle by members of the parish, and the altar is prepared. A collection is usually taken at this time.

Visitors are welcome to simply pass the basket along.

Stand

16. “Pray, brethren”

The priest invites everyone to pray that the sacrifice will be acceptable to God. The people stand and answer with a short prayer printed in the missal, then the priest prays over the gifts.

All
Answer with the prayer in the missal; then “Amen.”
Stand

17. “Lift up your hearts”

The great prayer of thanksgiving begins with a dialogue between the priest and the people that Christians have used since the earliest centuries.

Priest
The Lord be with you.
All
And with your spirit.
Priest
Lift up your hearts.
All
We lift them up to the Lord.
Priest
Let us give thanks to the Lord our God.
All
It is right and just.
Stand

18. Holy, Holy, Holy

The priest prays the Preface, praising God, and everyone joins in singing the “Holy, Holy, Holy” (Sanctus), echoing the angels’ song in Isaiah 6.

All
Sing along from the worship aid.

When it ends, everyone kneels.

Kneel

19. Eucharistic Prayer

This is the heart of the Mass. The priest repeats Jesus’ words at the Last Supper over the bread and wine. Catholics believe that at this moment they truly become the Body and Blood of Christ. The priest raises each one for all to see, and bells are often rung.

Many Catholics look at the Host as it is raised and silently pray, “My Lord and my God.” If you cannot kneel, sit forward quietly.

Kneel

20. The mystery of faith

The priest says “The mystery of faith,” and the people respond with a short acclamation about Christ’s death and Resurrection, printed in the missal.

All
Answer with the acclamation in the missal.
Kneel

21. The Great Amen

The Eucharistic Prayer ends with the priest lifting up the Body and Blood of Christ and praising the Father through Christ. The people answer with a sung “Amen,” often repeated.

All
Amen.

Then everyone stands.

Communion Rite

Stand

22. The Lord’s Prayer

Everyone prays the Our Father together. In some parishes people hold hands or raise their hands; in others they fold them. Do whatever feels comfortable.

All
Our Father, who art in heaven… but deliver us from evil.
Pause here while the priest prays alone.
All
For the kingdom, the power and the glory are yours, now and for ever.

Catholics don’t add “For thine is the kingdom” right after “deliver us from evil.” It comes a moment later, after the priest’s short prayer.

Stand

23. Sign of peace

The priest may invite everyone to offer one another a sign of peace. Shake hands, nod or wave to the people near you.

You
Peace be with you.

Keep it short and simple, just the people right around you.

Stand

24. Lamb of God

The priest breaks the consecrated Host while everyone sings the “Lamb of God.”

All
Lamb of God, you take away the sins of the world: have mercy on us. (twice)
All
Lamb of God, you take away the sins of the world: grant us peace.

Then everyone kneels.

Kneel

25. “Behold the Lamb of God”

The priest holds up the Host and invites the people to Communion. Everyone answers with the words of the Roman centurion in Matthew 8:8.

Priest
Behold the Lamb of God…
All
Lord, I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof, but only say the word and my soul shall be healed.
As you wish

26. Holy Communion

Catholics who are prepared go forward row by row. If you are not Catholic, you are welcome to stay in your pew and pray, letting others step past you. In many parishes you may also come forward with your arms crossed over your chest to receive a blessing.

Minister
The Body of Christ.
Catholic receiving
Amen.

Why can’t everyone receive? Catholics believe Communion is truly Christ and a sign of full unity in the faith, so it is received by Catholics who are prepared. Becoming Catholic is how that door opens.

Kneel

27. Quiet prayer

After Communion, people return to their pews and pray quietly, kneeling or sitting. A hymn is often sung.

A good moment to simply talk to God in your own words.

Stand

28. Prayer after Communion

The priest says “Let us pray” and everyone stands for a closing prayer. Parish announcements may follow.

All
Amen.

Concluding Rites

Stand

29. Blessing and dismissal

The priest blesses everyone. Make the Sign of the Cross as he does. Then he or the deacon sends the people out.

Priest
The Lord be with you.
All
And with your spirit.
Priest
…the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit.
All
Amen.
Priest or deacon
Go forth, the Mass is ended.
All
Thanks be to God.
As you wish

30. After Mass

A closing hymn is usually sung as the priest processes out. It’s polite to stay until he has left. Many people then kneel for a moment of prayer before leaving.

The priest often greets people at the door. Telling him “It was my first Mass” is a wonderful way to start a conversation. When you’re ready, see Start Here.

A few more things to know

  • How long is Mass? About an hour on Sundays, and about 30 minutes on weekdays.
  • What should I wear? Something you’d wear to a nice dinner is typical, but come as you are.
  • Can I bring children? Yes. Many churches have a cry room, but most parishes are happy to hear little ones.
  • Do I have to give money? No. The collection supports the parish. Visitors are welcome to let the basket pass.
  • Where do I find Mass times? Use Find a Church, then check the parish website or call.
