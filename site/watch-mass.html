Sick or homebound?

Does watching count for Sunday Mass?

Watching Mass on TV or online is a good way to pray, but it does not take the place of going to Mass. Catholics are asked to take part in Mass in person on Sundays and holy days of obligation.

You are excused when you have a serious reason you can’t go: if you are sick, homebound, recovering, frail with age, caring for someone who needs you, or when travel or weather makes it truly unsafe (Catechism 2181). There is no sin in missing Mass for a reason like that, and you don’t need anyone’s permission. Your pastor can also excuse you in other cases, so ask if you aren’t sure.

On those days, watching a Mass and making a Spiritual Communion is a beautiful way to keep the Lord’s Day.

See this year’s holy days of obligation.

Communion at home

Every parish can bring Holy Communion to people who can’t come to church. A priest, deacon or trained lay minister visits, often every week, and you don’t need to be registered at the parish to ask.

If you are sick, you can also ask for a priest to hear your Confession and give the Anointing of the Sick at home or in the hospital.

Call the parish office and say something like:

“Hi, my name is ____. I’m (my mother is) homebound at ____ and would like to receive Communion at home. Could someone from the parish come? I’d also like to see a priest for Confession.”

  • In a hospital or nursing home, ask the front desk for the Catholic chaplain.
  • The one-hour fast before Communion doesn’t bind the elderly, the sick or those caring for them (Canon 919).
  • Prepare a small table with a clean cloth, a crucifix and a candle if you have them.

Spiritual Communion prayer

When you can’t receive the Eucharist, you can still ask Jesus to come into your heart. Many people pray this at Communion time while watching Mass. It is adapted from St. Alphonsus Liguori.

My Jesus, I believe that you are present in the Most Holy Sacrament. I love you above all things, and I desire to receive you into my soul. Since I cannot at this moment receive you sacramentally, come at least spiritually into my heart. I embrace you as if you were already there, and I unite myself wholly to you. Never permit me to be separated from you. Amen.

Praying the Mass at home

Watch it live if you can

A live Mass draws you into the same prayer as the people in church. Replays are good too, especially for people who work or sleep at odd hours.

Make a little space

Turn off other screens, light a candle, put a crucifix or holy picture near the TV, and dress as you would for church if you’re able.

Pray along

Say the responses, sing, and stand, sit and kneel if you can. Our Mass companion has every response, and the day’s readings are here.

At Communion, pray

Pause, bow your head and pray the Spiritual Communion prayer. Stay quiet for a moment of thanksgiving.

Stay connected

Call your parish: priests and ministers want to know who can’t come, and many parishes deliver the bulletin or connect you with a visitor.
