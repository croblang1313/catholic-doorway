Family Corner

Simple ways to live the Church year at home, season by season, plus printable sheets for First Communion and Confirmation.

Advent · Christmas · Lent · Easter · All year long · Sacrament prep sheets

Advent Now

The four weeks before Christmas are a time of hopeful waiting. Keep things simple and quiet so Christmas feels new.

Light an Advent wreath

Four candles, three purple and one pink, in a ring of greens. Light one more each Sunday and say a short prayer before dinner. See the Advent wreath prayers.

Make a Jesse tree

Each day, read a short Bible story from Adam and Eve to the birth of Jesus, and hang a paper ornament for it on a branch.

Celebrate St. Nicholas Day

On the night of December 5, children put out their shoes. In the morning they find a small treat and hear the story of Saint Nicholas.

Fill the manger with straw

Set out an empty manger. Each kind act adds a piece of straw or yarn, so baby Jesus has a soft bed by Christmas.

Christmas Now

Christmas is not one day but a whole season, from Christmas Eve to the Baptism of the Lord in January.

Bless your Nativity set

Say a prayer over your crèche together, and let the youngest child place baby Jesus in the manger on Christmas Eve.

Move the Wise Men

Put the three kings across the room and move them a little closer to the manger each day until Epiphany.

Chalk your door

At Epiphany, write 20 + C + M + B + 27 above your front door. The letters stand for the Wise Men and for “May Christ bless this house.”

Keep celebrating

Leave the tree up and keep singing carols. The world moves on after December 25; the Church keeps celebrating.

Lent Now

Forty days of prayer, fasting and giving that get our hearts ready for Easter.

A coin jar for the poor

Put a coin in a jar whenever you give up a treat. At Easter, give the money to a food pantry or to your parish’s poor box.

Bean jar of kindness

Drop a dried bean in a jar for each kind act or sacrifice. On Easter morning, the beans have turned into jelly beans!

Pray the Stations on Fridays

Walk the Stations of the Cross as a family at church or at home, and have a simple meatless dinner.

A crown of thorns

Make a ring of salt dough with toothpicks stuck in it. Pull out one thorn for each good deed. On Easter, fill the holes with flowers.

Easter Now

Easter lasts fifty days, all the way to Pentecost. That is longer than Lent!

Say Alleluia at every meal

Alleluia is not said during Lent, so bring it back loudly: say it in grace before every meal of the Easter season.

Resurrection eggs

Fill twelve plastic eggs with small objects that tell the Easter story (a palm leaf, a coin, a cross, a stone) and open one each day of Holy Week, saving the empty one for Easter.

Bless your Easter basket

Many parishes bless Easter food on Holy Saturday. Bring a basket with bread, eggs and something sweet.

Wear red for Pentecost

Pentecost ends the Easter season. Wear red and cut out paper flames with the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit written on them.

All year long Now

Ordinary Time is the season of growing. Here are habits that work any time of year.

Make a home prayer table

A small table or shelf with a cross, a Bible, a candle and a cloth in the color of the season. Gather there for family prayer.

Celebrate baptism days

Light your baptismal candle on the anniversary of each child’s Baptism and look at the photos together.

Keep saints’ days

Celebrate the feast of each child’s patron saint with a favorite dessert and a saint story.

Bless your children

At bedtime, trace a small cross on your child’s forehead and say, “God bless you.”

Make Sunday special

Go to Mass together, then share pancakes or a special lunch and talk about what you heard in the Gospel.

Crown Mary in May

In May, put flowers by a picture or statue of Mary, and pray a decade of the Rosary together.
