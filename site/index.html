Where are you on the journey?

Pick the one that fits you best. Each path is a few simple steps, in order.

Curious about becoming Catholic

No pressure and no sign-up. This is the usual order.

  1. Go to a Sunday Mass and just watchAnyone is welcome. Sit toward the back; our phone guide tells you what happens.
  2. Bring your questionsShort, honest answers about Mary, confession, the Pope and more.
  3. Learn how OCIA worksThe Church's path for adults: its four stages, how long it takes, and what it costs (nothing).
  4. Call a parish and ask about OCIAFind the parish nearest you and ask for the OCIA or RCIA coordinator.

When: OCIA groups usually begin in late summer or fall, but you can call a parish any time of year.

Coming back to the faith

Coming home is simpler than you might think.

  1. Read how coming back worksNo paperwork or classes are needed for most people. Just come.
  2. Prepare for ConfessionA gentle examination of conscience to help you remember what to say.
  3. Go to ConfessionStep by step, including what to say if it's been years. Parishes list Confession times online.
  4. Join a parishRegister so the parish can help with sacraments, from weddings to funerals.

When: Any time. Many parishes add extra Confession times during Advent and Lent.

Growing in daily prayer

Build a simple daily rhythm, one habit at a time.

  1. Start the day with the Mass readingsToday's readings, with audio, from the U.S. bishops.
  2. Pray the RosaryStart with one decade; the guide shows today's mysteries.
  3. Pick a reading planThe Bible in a Year, the Catechism in a Year, or the Gospels in 30 days.
  4. Add devotions with remindersThe Angelus at noon, the Divine Mercy Chaplet at 3 p.m., and novenas.
  5. End the day with an examenA few quiet minutes to look back on the day with God.

When: Readings in the morning, the Angelus at noon, Divine Mercy at 3 p.m., and the examen before bed.

Raising kids in the faith

Small, joyful habits at home make the biggest difference.

  1. Teach first prayersLarge type with read-aloud, from the Sign of the Cross to bedtime prayers.
  2. Get ready for Mass togetherA picture guide to what happens, plus an I-spy game for church.
  3. Read a saint story at bedtimeShort true stories, each with something to try.
  4. Live the seasons at homeSimple ideas for Advent, Christmas, Lent and Easter, plus sacrament prep sheets.
  5. Color on a rainy dayFree saints, Bible stories and seasons to print or color on a tablet.

When: Most parishes begin First Communion preparation around age 7 (second grade). Ask your parish a year ahead.

Preparing for a sacrament

What to do, and how far ahead to call the parish.

  1. Getting marriedCall the parish at least six months to a year before the date you hope for.
  2. Baptizing a babyCall during pregnancy or soon after the birth; most parishes have a short class.
  3. First Communion or ConfirmationUsually a one- or two-year program. Register in late summer.
  4. Going to ConfessionNo appointment needed during the parish's Confession times.
  5. Someone is seriously illCall the parish right away, day or night, for Anointing of the Sick.
  6. Planning a funeralCall the parish or funeral home first; they will help with the rest.

When: When someone is near death, call a priest at once. For everything else, call the parish office during the week.
