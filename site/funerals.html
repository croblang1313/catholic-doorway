Planning a Catholic funeral

We are sorry for your loss. A Catholic funeral commends the person to God’s mercy, comforts those who mourn, and proclaims the hope of the resurrection. Here is what to expect, and how to plan.

First steps

  1. Call a funeral home

    Tell them you would like a Catholic funeral and which parish. The funeral director usually contacts the parish to set the date. You can also call the parish yourself.

  2. Meet with the parish

    A priest, deacon or funeral ministry volunteer will help you choose the readings, music and any family participation, such as readers and people to bring up the gifts.

  3. Arrange the burial

    Catholic cemeteries are common, but any cemetery is fine. The priest or deacon can bless the grave.

The three parts of a Catholic funeral

1. The Vigil (the wake)

Usually the evening before, at the funeral home or church: a short service of Scripture and prayer, often with a Rosary. This is a good place for longer memories and stories from family and friends.

2. The Funeral Mass

The center of the funeral. The body (or the cremated remains) is received at the church door and the casket is covered with a white cloth (a pall) that recalls Baptism. Readings, a homily, the Eucharist, and a final commendation follow. Many dioceses allow a short “words of remembrance” by one family member near the end; ask your parish about its guidelines. A funeral can also be held without Mass when that is more suitable.

3. The Committal

Brief prayers at the graveside or mausoleum as the body or ashes are laid to rest.

Common questions

Is cremation allowed?

Yes. The Church prefers that the body be present for the funeral Mass with cremation afterward, but cremation before the funeral is allowed too. The ashes should be treated with the same respect as a body: buried or placed in a columbarium or mausoleum. The Church asks that ashes not be scattered, divided among family, kept at home, or made into jewelry.

My loved one had stopped practicing. Can they have a Catholic funeral?

Usually yes. Talk with the priest. The funeral is a prayer for God’s mercy, which is exactly what we all need.

Can a non-Catholic family member have a funeral in a Catholic church?

Sometimes, for example a non-Catholic spouse of a Catholic. The priest can ask the bishop. Talk with the parish.

What does it cost?

The parish may have a set fee or suggested offering for the church, musicians and staff. Funeral home and cemetery costs are separate.

How can we keep praying for them?

Ask a parish to offer a Mass for the deceased. Call the office, give the person’s name and a few dates that suit you, and it will be listed in the bulletin. A small offering (often about $10) is customary. Many families have a Mass said on the anniversary of the death, and the Church especially remembers the dead on All Souls’ Day, November 2. You can also pray the Eternal Rest prayer.

Can I plan my own funeral?

Yes, and it is a gift to your family. Write down your wishes for readings, music and burial, and give a copy to your family and your parish.
