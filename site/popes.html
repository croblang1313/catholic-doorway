The Popes, from Peter to Leo XIV

For nearly two thousand years the Bishop of Rome has succeeded St. Peter in an unbroken line. Here are all 267 Popes in order, with their years as Pope and what each is remembered for.

1st 2nd 3rd 4th 5th 6th 7th 8th 9th 10th 11th 12th 13th 14th 15th 16th 17th 18th 19th 20th 21st

The number is each Pope’s place in the line of succession. Dates for the first centuries follow the Church’s traditional list and are approximate (“c.” means “about”); historians differ on them by a few years. Benedict IX served three separate times, so he is counted three times, as the Vatican’s list does.

No popes match that search.

1st century

  1. 1

    St. Peter

    Pope c. 30s – 64 or 67Martyred in Rome, 64 or 67

    A Galilean fisherman called by Jesus, who named him the rock on which he would build his Church. Leader of the Apostles, he preached in Jerusalem, Antioch and Rome, where he was crucified under Nero.

  2. 2

    St. Linus

    Pope c. 67–c. 76Died c. 76

    Named by early writers as Peter's first successor in Rome. He may be the Linus greeted by St. Paul in 2 Timothy 4:21.

  3. 3

    St. Anacletus

    Pope c. 76–c. 88Died c. 88

    Also called Cletus. One of the earliest bishops of Rome, remembered in the Roman Canon of the Mass. Tradition holds that he died a martyr.

  4. 4

    St. Clement I

    Pope c. 88–c. 97Died c. 97

    Author of the Letter of Clement to the Corinthians, one of the oldest Christian writings outside the New Testament, urging peace and order in that church.

  5. 5

    St. Evaristus

    Pope c. 97–c. 105Died c. 105

    An early bishop of Rome during the reign of Emperor Trajan. Little is known for certain about his life; tradition counts him a martyr.

2nd century

  1. 6

    St. Alexander I

    Pope c. 105–c. 115Died c. 115

    Led the Roman church in the early 2nd century. Later tradition credits him with introducing the use of blessed water mixed with salt.

  2. 7

    St. Sixtus I

    Pope c. 115–c. 125Died c. 125

    An early 2nd-century bishop of Rome, remembered among the martyrs. He is often identified with the Sixtus named in early lists of Roman bishops.

  3. 8

    St. Telesphorus

    Pope c. 125–c. 136Died c. 136

    The first bishop of Rome after Peter whose martyrdom is attested by St. Irenaeus. Tradition links him to the custom of Christmas night Masses.

  4. 9

    St. Hyginus

    Pope c. 136–c. 140Died c. 140

    Led the Roman church as Gnostic teachers such as Valentinus and Cerdo arrived in Rome, challenging Christian teaching.

  5. 10

    St. Pius I

    Pope c. 140–c. 155Died c. 155

    Brother of Hermas, author of The Shepherd. During his time the heretic Marcion was expelled from the Roman church.

  6. 11

    St. Anicetus

    Pope c. 155–c. 166Died c. 166

    Welcomed St. Polycarp of Smyrna to Rome. The two disagreed over the date of Easter but parted in peace and charity.

  7. 12

    St. Soter

    Pope c. 166–c. 175Died c. 175

    Known for the generosity of the Roman church, which sent aid to Christians in need, praised in a letter by Dionysius of Corinth.

  8. 13

    St. Eleutherius

    Pope c. 175–c. 189Died c. 189

    Led the Church as the Montanist movement spread. St. Irenaeus wrote of him in his list of Roman bishops.

  9. 14

    St. Victor I

    Pope c. 189–c. 199Died 199

    The first bishop of Rome from North Africa. He pressed churches to celebrate Easter on Sunday, which became the universal practice.

  10. 15

    St. Zephyrinus

    Pope c. 199–217Died 217

    Guided the Church through debates about the Trinity, assisted by his deacon Callixtus, who managed the Christian cemetery on the Appian Way.

3rd century

  1. 16

    St. Callixtus I

    Pope c. 218–c. 222Died 222

    A former slave who rose to lead the Church. He taught that sinners who repent may be readmitted to communion, and he died a martyr.

  2. 17

    St. Urban I

    Pope c. 222–230Died 230

    Led the Church during a relatively peaceful period under Emperor Alexander Severus.

  3. 18

    St. Pontian

    Pope 230–235Died 235

    Exiled to the mines of Sardinia under Emperor Maximinus Thrax, he resigned so the Church could choose a new leader, the first recorded papal resignation.

  4. 19

    St. Anterus

    Pope 235–236Died 236

    Reigned only about six weeks. Tradition says he collected the records of the martyrs. He was the first pope buried in the Papal Crypt of the Catacomb of St. Callixtus.

  5. 20

    St. Fabian

    Pope 236–250Died 250

    A layman chosen, according to Eusebius, when a dove settled on his head. He organized Rome into districts for the care of the poor and was martyred under Decius.

  6. 21

    St. Cornelius

    Pope 251–253Died 253

    Elected after persecution, he welcomed back Christians who had lapsed and repented, opposing the rigorist Novatian. He died in exile.

  7. 22

    St. Lucius I

    Pope 253–254Died 254

    Briefly exiled at the start of his reign, he continued Cornelius's merciful policy toward the lapsed. St. Cyprian praised him.

  8. 23

    St. Stephen I

    Pope 254–257Died 257

    Taught that baptism by heretics is valid if done in the name of the Trinity, so converts need not be rebaptized, a view later confirmed by the Church.

  9. 24

    St. Sixtus II

    Pope 257–258Died 258

    Beheaded with his deacons while celebrating the liturgy in the catacombs during the persecution of Valerian. St. Lawrence was martyred days later.

  10. 25

    St. Dionysius

    Pope 259–268Died 268

    Rebuilt the Roman church after the persecution of Valerian and organized parishes. He sent aid to Christians in Cappadocia captured by invaders.

  11. 26

    St. Felix I

    Pope 269–274Died 274

    Wrote on the doctrine of the Trinity and the Incarnation. His reign fell under Emperor Aurelian.

  12. 27

    St. Eutychian

    Pope 275–283Died 283

    Led the Church in a time of relative peace. Little else is known for certain.

  13. 28

    St. Caius

    Pope 283–296Died 296

    Reigned in the years before the Great Persecution under Diocletian. Tradition says he was related to the emperor.

  14. 29

    St. Marcellinus

    Pope 296–304Died c. 304

    Pope at the start of Diocletian's Great Persecution, the most severe the Church endured.

4th century

  1. 30

    St. Marcellus I

    Pope c. 308–309Died 309

    Elected after a long vacancy, he reorganized the Roman church after the persecution but was exiled over disputes about readmitting the lapsed.

  2. 31

    St. Eusebius

    Pope 309Died c. 310

    Reigned only a few months before being exiled to Sicily by Emperor Maxentius amid unrest over the treatment of the lapsed.

  3. 32

    St. Miltiades

    Pope 311–314Died 314

    The first pope of the age of toleration. After the Edict of Milan (313) the empire returned church property, and he received the Lateran palace.

  4. 33

    St. Sylvester I

    Pope 314–c. 336Died c. 336

    Led the Church during the reign of Constantine, when the Council of Nicaea (325) defined the divinity of Christ and the first great Roman basilicas were built.

  5. 34

    St. Mark

    Pope 336Died 336

    Reigned less than a year. He is credited with founding the Basilica of San Marco in Rome.

  6. 35

    St. Julius I

    Pope 337–352Died 352

    Defended St. Athanasius against the Arians and affirmed the faith of Nicaea at the Council of Sardica.

  7. 36

    Liberius

    Pope 352–366Died 366

    Exiled by the Arian Emperor Constantius II for refusing to condemn Athanasius. Tradition links him to the founding of St. Mary Major.

  8. 37

    St. Damasus I

    Pope 366–384Died 384

    Commissioned St. Jerome to revise the Latin Bible, which became the Vulgate. He honored the martyrs by restoring the catacombs with inscriptions.

  9. 38

    St. Siricius

    Pope 384–399Died 399

    Issued the earliest surviving papal decretal, giving binding answers on Church discipline to the bishops of Spain.

  10. 39

    St. Anastasius I

    Pope 399–401Died 401

    Condemned certain teachings of Origen. St. Jerome praised him as a man of holy life.

5th century

  1. 40

    St. Innocent I

    Pope c. 402–417Died 417

    Pope when Alaric's Visigoths sacked Rome in 410. He firmly asserted Rome's authority and condemned Pelagianism.

  2. 41

    St. Zosimus

    Pope 417–418Died 418

    A Greek who at first hesitated over the Pelagian question, then condemned Pelagianism in his letter called the Tractoria.

  3. 42

    St. Boniface I

    Pope 418–422Died 422

    His election was contested by the antipope Eulalius. He defended the authority of Rome over the churches of Illyricum.

  4. 43

    St. Celestine I

    Pope 422–432Died 432

    Sent bishops to the Council of Ephesus (431), which proclaimed Mary the Mother of God. He sent Palladius to the Christians of Ireland.

  5. 44

    St. Sixtus III

    Pope 432–440Died 440

    Rebuilt the Basilica of St. Mary Major to honor Mary as Mother of God after Ephesus, and worked to reconcile the churches of Antioch and Alexandria.

  6. 45

    St. Leo I (the Great)

    Pope 440–461Died 461

    His Tome shaped the Council of Chalcedon's teaching that Christ is one person in two natures. He met Attila the Hun and persuaded him to turn back from Rome. A Doctor of the Church.

  7. 46

    St. Hilarius

    Pope 461–468Died 468

    As papal legate he had escaped the "Robber Council" of Ephesus. As pope he strengthened Church order in Gaul and Spain and built churches in Rome.

  8. 47

    St. Simplicius

    Pope 468–483Died 483

    Reigned when the last Western Roman emperor was deposed in 476. He defended the Council of Chalcedon against the Monophysites.

  9. 48

    St. Felix III

    Pope 483–492Died 492

    Excommunicated the Patriarch of Constantinople over the Henotikon, beginning the Acacian Schism between Rome and the East. An ancestor of St. Gregory the Great.

  10. 49

    St. Gelasius I

    Pope 492–496Died 496

    Born in Roman Africa, he wrote that the world is governed by two powers, the sacred authority of bishops and the royal power of kings. Known for his charity to the poor.

  11. 50

    Anastasius II

    Pope 496–498Died 498

    Sought to heal the Acacian Schism with Constantinople through conciliation.

  12. 51

    St. Symmachus

    Pope 498–514Died 514

    Overcame a long challenge from the rival claimant Laurentius. He supported the Catholics of Africa persecuted by the Arian Vandals.

6th century

  1. 52

    St. Hormisdas

    Pope 514–523Died 523

    Ended the Acacian Schism in 519 with the Formula of Hormisdas, signed by the Eastern bishops. His son later became Pope St. Silverius.

  2. 53

    St. John I

    Pope 523–526Died 526

    Sent to Constantinople by the Arian king Theodoric. On his return he was imprisoned at Ravenna, where he died, and he is honored as a martyr.

  3. 54

    St. Felix IV

    Pope 526–530Died 530

    Appointed under the influence of King Theodoric. He built the Church of Sts. Cosmas and Damian in the Roman Forum.

  4. 55

    Boniface II

    Pope 530–532Died 532

    The first pope of Germanic descent. He confirmed the Council of Orange's teaching on grace against Semi-Pelagianism.

  5. 56

    John II

    Born Mercurius

    Pope 533–535Died 535

    The first pope known to have taken a new name on election, since his birth name honored the pagan god Mercury.

  6. 57

    St. Agapetus I

    Pope 535–536Died 536

    Traveled to Constantinople, where he deposed the Monophysite patriarch Anthimus and consecrated a successor. He died there.

  7. 58

    St. Silverius

    Pope 536–537Died 537

    Son of Pope Hormisdas. He was deposed through the intrigues of Empress Theodora and died in exile. He is honored as a martyr.

  8. 59

    Vigilius

    Pope 537–555Died 555

    Held for years in Constantinople by Emperor Justinian during the dispute over the Three Chapters, around the Second Council of Constantinople (553).

  9. 60

    Pelagius I

    Pope c. 556–561Died 561

    Rebuilt the Church's finances and relief of the poor in an Italy devastated by the Gothic Wars.

  10. 61

    John III

    Born Catelinus

    Pope 561–574Died 574

    Led the Church as the Lombards invaded Italy. He completed the Church of the Holy Apostles in Rome.

  11. 62

    Benedict I

    Pope 575–579Died 579

    Reigned during the Lombard invasions, as famine and plague struck Rome.

  12. 63

    Pelagius II

    Pope 579–590Died 590

    Sought help from Constantinople against the Lombards. He died of the plague that swept Rome.

  13. 64

    St. Gregory I (the Great)

    Pope 590–604Lived c. 540–604

    A monk who reluctantly became pope. He sent St. Augustine to convert the English, cared for Rome's poor, reformed the liturgy and wrote the Pastoral Rule. A Doctor of the Church.

7th century

  1. 65

    Sabinian

    Pope 604–606Died 606

    Successor of Gregory the Great. He sold the Church's grain during a famine, which made him unpopular.

  2. 66

    Boniface III

    Pope 607Died 607

    Obtained from Emperor Phocas recognition of the Roman church as head of all the churches.

  3. 67

    St. Boniface IV

    Pope 608–615Died 615

    Converted the Pantheon in Rome into a church dedicated to Mary and all the martyrs, a step toward the feast of All Saints.

  4. 68

    St. Adeodatus I

    Pope 615–618Died 618

    Also called Deusdedit. A Roman priest of long service, he cared for the clergy and the sick during an earthquake and epidemic.

  5. 69

    Boniface V

    Pope 619–625Died 625

    Supported the mission to England and corresponded with King Edwin of Northumbria.

  6. 70

    Honorius I

    Pope 625–638Died 638

    Promoted missions in England. He was later condemned by the Third Council of Constantinople for his ambiguous letters on Monothelitism.

  7. 71

    Severinus

    Pope 640Died 640

    His confirmation was delayed more than a year because he refused to accept the Monothelite Ecthesis. He reigned only two months.

  8. 72

    John IV

    Pope 640–642Died 642

    A Dalmatian who condemned Monothelitism and ransomed captives taken in his homeland.

  9. 73

    Theodore I

    Pope 642–649Died 649

    A Greek from Jerusalem who opposed Monothelitism and excommunicated the Monothelite patriarch Pyrrhus.

  10. 74

    St. Martin I

    Pope 649–655Died 655

    Condemned Monothelitism at the Lateran Synod of 649. Arrested by order of Emperor Constans II, he died in exile in Crimea, the last pope honored as a martyr.

  11. 75

    St. Eugene I

    Pope c. 656–657Died 657

    Elected while Martin I was in exile. He stood firm against pressure to accept Monothelitism.

  12. 76

    St. Vitalian

    Pope 657–672Died 672

    Sent Theodore of Tarsus as Archbishop of Canterbury, who organized the church in England.

  13. 77

    Adeodatus II

    Pope 672–676Died 676

    A Roman monk known for kindness to pilgrims. He restored churches in Rome.

  14. 78

    Donus

    Pope 676–678Died 678

    Restored Roman churches and reconciled the church of Ravenna to Rome.

  15. 79

    St. Agatho

    Pope 678–681Died 681

    A Sicilian whose letter guided the Third Council of Constantinople (680–681), which condemned Monothelitism.

  16. 80

    St. Leo II

    Pope 682–683Died 683

    Confirmed the Third Council of Constantinople. He was noted as a preacher and musician.

  17. 81

    St. Benedict II

    Pope 684–685Died 685

    Obtained the emperor's agreement that a pope could be consecrated without waiting for imperial approval.

  18. 82

    John V

    Pope 685–686Died 686

    The first of a series of Eastern popes of this era, born in Syria. He was known for his generosity.

  19. 83

    Conon

    Pope 686–687Died 687

    An elderly compromise candidate, son of a soldier from Thrace. He reigned less than a year.

  20. 84

    St. Sergius I

    Pope 687–701Died 701

    Introduced the Agnus Dei into the Mass, refused to accept the decrees of the Council in Trullo, and baptized King Caedwalla of Wessex.

8th century

  1. 85

    John VI

    Pope 701–705Died 705

    A Greek who ransomed captives taken by the Lombards and protected Rome.

  2. 86

    John VII

    Pope 705–707Died 707

    A Greek who decorated Roman churches with fine mosaics and frescoes.

  3. 87

    Sisinnius

    Pope 708Died 708

    Suffered severely from gout and reigned only about three weeks.

  4. 88

    Constantine

    Pope 708–715Died 715

    The last pope to visit Constantinople before Paul VI in 1967, where he was received with honor by Emperor Justinian II.

  5. 89

    St. Gregory II

    Pope 715–731Died 731

    Commissioned St. Boniface as missionary to the Germans and opposed Emperor Leo III's iconoclasm.

  6. 90

    St. Gregory III

    Pope 731–741Died 741

    Born in Syria, the last pope to seek the Byzantine emperor's approval. He condemned iconoclasm and supported St. Boniface's missions.

  7. 91

    St. Zachary

    Pope 741–752Died 752

    The last Greek pope of the period. He approved Pepin's anointing as King of the Franks and negotiated peace with the Lombards.

  8. 92

    Stephen II

    Pope 752–757Died 757

    Crossed the Alps to seek Frankish help against the Lombards. Pepin's donation of land laid the foundation of the Papal States.

  9. 93

    St. Paul I

    Pope 757–767Died 767

    Brother of Stephen II. He strengthened the alliance with Pepin and moved relics from the catacombs into Rome's churches.

  10. 94

    Stephen III

    Pope 768–772Died 772

    Held the Lateran Council of 769, which set rules for papal elections and condemned iconoclasm.

  11. 95

    Adrian I

    Pope 772–795Died 795

    Allied with Charlemagne, who defeated the Lombards. His legates took part in the Second Council of Nicaea (787), which restored the veneration of icons.

  12. 96

    St. Leo III

    Pope 796–816Died 816

    Crowned Charlemagne emperor on Christmas Day 800, founding the Holy Roman Empire.

9th century

  1. 97

    Stephen IV

    Pope 816–817Died 817

    Traveled to Reims to crown Louis the Pious as emperor.

  2. 98

    St. Paschal I

    Pope 817–824Died 824

    Built and decorated churches in Rome, including Santa Prassede, and gave refuge to Greek monks fleeing iconoclasm.

  3. 99

    Eugene II

    Pope 824–827Died 827

    Accepted the Constitutio Romana, defining the emperor's role in Rome. He promoted education for clergy.

  4. 100

    Valentine

    Pope 827Died 827

    A Roman deacon praised for his piety. He died after a reign of about a month.

  5. 101

    Gregory IV

    Pope 827–844Died 844

    Tried to mediate in the civil wars of Louis the Pious and his sons. He fortified Ostia against Saracen raids.

  6. 102

    Sergius II

    Pope c. 844–847Died 847

    Reigned when Saracen raiders sacked St. Peter's Basilica outside the walls of Rome in 846.

  7. 103

    St. Leo IV

    Pope 847–855Died 855

    Built the Leonine Wall around St. Peter's and the Vatican after the Saracen raid, and led the defense of Rome at the Battle of Ostia.

  8. 104

    Benedict III

    Pope 855–858Died 858

    His election was briefly challenged by the antipope Anastasius. He worked to help churches in England and the East.

  9. 105

    St. Nicholas I (the Great)

    Pope 858–867Died 867

    A strong pope who upheld the indissolubility of marriage against King Lothair II and asserted papal authority in the dispute over Patriarch Photius.

  10. 106

    Adrian II

    Pope 867–872Died 872

    Approved the Slavonic liturgy of Sts. Cyril and Methodius. His legates attended the Fourth Council of Constantinople.

  11. 107

    John VIII

    Pope 872–882Died 882

    Defended Rome against Saracen raids and supported St. Methodius. He was the first pope to be assassinated.

  12. 108

    Marinus I

    Pope 882–884Died 884

    Had served as papal legate to Constantinople. He was the first pope known to have been bishop of another diocese before his election.

  13. 109

    St. Adrian III

    Pope 884–885Died c. 885

    Reigned about a year amid factional violence in Rome. He died on his way to an imperial diet.

  14. 110

    Stephen V

    Pope 885–891Died 891

    Fed Rome during famine and drought. He forbade the Slavonic liturgy after Methodius's death.

  15. 111

    Formosus

    Pope 891–896Died 896

    A former missionary to Bulgaria. After his death his body was exhumed and put on trial by Stephen VI in the infamous "Cadaver Synod."

  16. 112

    Boniface VI

    Pope 896Died 896

    Reigned only about two weeks amid the turmoil after Formosus's death.

  17. 113

    Stephen VI

    Pope 896–897Died 897

    Held the Cadaver Synod against Formosus's corpse. Public outrage led to his imprisonment and death.

  18. 114

    Romanus

    Pope 897Died 897

    Reigned about four months during the turbulent years after the Cadaver Synod.

  19. 115

    Theodore II

    Pope 897Died 897

    In a reign of about twenty days, he reburied Formosus with honor and restored those he had ordained.

  20. 116

    John IX

    Pope 898–900Died 900

    Confirmed the rehabilitation of Formosus and annulled the Cadaver Synod.

  21. 117

    Benedict IV

    Pope 900–903Died 903

    Crowned Louis III of Provence as emperor. He was praised for his charity.

10th century

  1. 118

    Leo V

    Pope 903–904Died c. 905

    Overthrown after about a month and imprisoned. He likely died in prison.

  2. 119

    Sergius III

    Pope 904–911Died 911

    Reigned at the start of the period when Rome's aristocratic families dominated the papacy. He rebuilt the Lateran Basilica after an earthquake.

  3. 120

    Anastasius III

    Pope 911–913Died c. 913

    Reigned about two years while the powerful Theophylact family controlled Rome.

  4. 121

    Lando

    Pope 913–c. 914Died 914

    Reigned about seven months. His name has never been used by another pope.

  5. 122

    John X

    Pope 914–928Died c. 929

    Organized the Christian alliance that defeated the Saracens at the Garigliano (915). He was deposed and died in prison.

  6. 123

    Leo VI

    Pope 928Died 928

    Reigned about seven months while Marozia controlled Rome.

  7. 124

    Stephen VII

    Pope 929–931Died 931

    Reigned about two years under the influence of Marozia. He granted privileges to monasteries.

  8. 125

    John XI

    Pope 931–935Died c. 936

    Son of Marozia, elected young. He granted privileges to the reforming Abbey of Cluny.

  9. 126

    Leo VII

    Pope 936–939Died 939

    Supported monastic reform and invited St. Odo of Cluny to Rome.

  10. 127

    Stephen VIII

    Pope 939–942Died 942

    Reigned under the domination of Alberic II, ruler of Rome.

  11. 128

    Marinus II

    Pope 942–946Died 946

    Concentrated on the reform of clergy and monasteries.

  12. 129

    Agapetus II

    Pope 946–955Died 955

    Supported monastic reform and the expansion of the Church in Germany and Scandinavia.

  13. 130

    John XII

    Pope 955–964Lived c. 937–964

    Became pope as a teenager. He crowned Otto I emperor in 962, but his worldly life led Otto to have him deposed.

  14. 131

    Leo VIII

    Pope 963–965Died 965

    A layman installed by Emperor Otto I in 963, after a synod declared John XII deposed. His status was disputed, but he is counted in the official list.

  15. 132

    Benedict V

    Pope 964Died 965

    Elected by the Romans after John XII's death while Leo VIII was still claiming the office, he was deposed by Otto I and died in exile in Hamburg.

  16. 133

    John XIII

    Pope 965–972Died 972

    Crowned Otto II co-emperor and established new dioceses, including Magdeburg as a center for missions to the Slavs.

  17. 134

    Benedict VI

    Pope 973–974Died 974

    Murdered in prison by order of the Crescentii family.

  18. 135

    Benedict VII

    Pope 974–983Died 983

    Restored order in Rome, opposed simony and supported monastic reform.

  19. 136

    John XIV

    Born Pietro Canepanova

    Pope 983–984Died 984

    Chancellor of Emperor Otto II. After Otto died, he was imprisoned and died in the Castel Sant'Angelo.

  20. 137

    John XV

    Pope 985–996Died 996

    Carried out the first recorded formal canonization, of St. Ulrich of Augsburg, in 993.

  21. 138

    Gregory V

    Born Bruno of Carinthia

    Pope 996–999Lived c. 972–999

    The first German pope, a cousin of Emperor Otto III, elected in his mid-twenties.

  22. 139

    Sylvester II

    Born Gerbert of Aurillac

    Pope 999–1003Died 1003

    The first French pope and the greatest scholar of his age, who promoted mathematics and astronomy. He established the church in Poland and Hungary, sending a crown to St. Stephen.

11th century

  1. 140

    John XVII

    Pope 1003Died 1003

    Reigned about six months under the Crescentii family.

  2. 141

    John XVIII
