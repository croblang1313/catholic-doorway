The Divine Mercy Chaplet

A short prayer on ordinary rosary beads, asking for God’s mercy on us and on the whole world. Jesus taught it to St. Faustina Kowalska, a Polish nun, in the 1930s. It takes about ten minutes.

Pray it now

Bead by bead

Opens one step at a time in large print with Back and Next buttons. Your screen stays on and your place is saved.

When: any time, and especially at 3 p.m., the hour Jesus died, which is called the Hour of Mercy. It is also prayed each day of the Divine Mercy Novena, from Good Friday to Divine Mercy Sunday. Get a 3 p.m. reminder.

You need: a rosary, or just your fingers to count to ten.

1. The Sign of the Cross

Hold the crucifix and make the Sign of the Cross: In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

If you like, add this short prayer: O Blood and Water, which gushed forth from the Heart of Jesus as a fount of mercy for us, I trust in you.

2. Our Father, Hail Mary and the Creed

On the first three beads, pray one Our Father, one Hail Mary and the Apostles’ Creed.

The words are on the Prayers page.

3. Decade 1

On the large bead
Eternal Father, I offer you the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity of your dearly beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, in atonement for our sins and those of the whole world.
On each of the 10 small beads
For the sake of his sorrowful Passion, have mercy on us and on the whole world. (10 times)

4. Decade 2

On the large bead
Eternal Father, I offer you the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity of your dearly beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, in atonement for our sins and those of the whole world.
On each of the 10 small beads
For the sake of his sorrowful Passion, have mercy on us and on the whole world. (10 times)

5. Decade 3

On the large bead
Eternal Father, I offer you the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity of your dearly beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, in atonement for our sins and those of the whole world.
On each of the 10 small beads
For the sake of his sorrowful Passion, have mercy on us and on the whole world. (10 times)

6. Decade 4

On the large bead
Eternal Father, I offer you the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity of your dearly beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, in atonement for our sins and those of the whole world.
On each of the 10 small beads
For the sake of his sorrowful Passion, have mercy on us and on the whole world. (10 times)

7. Decade 5

On the large bead
Eternal Father, I offer you the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity of your dearly beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, in atonement for our sins and those of the whole world.
On each of the 10 small beads
For the sake of his sorrowful Passion, have mercy on us and on the whole world. (10 times)

8. Holy God

After the fifth decade, pray three times:

3 times
Holy God, Holy Mighty One, Holy Immortal One, have mercy on us and on the whole world.

9. Jesus, I trust in you

Make the Sign of the Cross. Many people end simply with the words St. Faustina asked to be written under the image of Divine Mercy: Jesus, I trust in you.

The chaplet takes about ten minutes. It is often prayed at 3 p.m., the hour Jesus died.

About Divine Mercy

St. Faustina (1905–1938) recorded in her diary that Jesus asked for an image of himself with rays of red and pale light streaming from his heart, signed Jesus, I trust in you, and for a feast of mercy on the Sunday after Easter. St. John Paul II canonized her in 2000 and named that day Divine Mercy Sunday for the whole Church. The heart of the devotion is simple: trust in God’s mercy, and show mercy to others.
